# וְאִם־אָמֹ֤ר יֹאמַר֙ הָעֶ֔בֶד אָהַ֙בְתִּי֙ אֶת־אֲדֹנִ֔י אֶת־אִשְׁתִּ֖י וְאֶת־בָּנָ֑י לֹ֥א אֵצֵ֖א חָפְשִֽׁי׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Accents.** The zaqef qatan on הָעֶ֔בֶד closes "if the slave indeed says." The atnach on וְאֶת־בָּנָ֑י divides the quoted sentence: "I love…" ǀ "I will not go out free." The speech is one sentence with its main pause before the conclusion; v. 6 gives the legal consequence. The zaqef on אֲדֹנִ֔י is the usual accent on the first item of such a list (cf. Gen 11:26, 27; Ex 35:11; Lev 1:8).

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**אָמֹר יֹאמַר.** Infinitive absolute before a finite verb of the same root: "if the slave indeed says."
- **Frequency.** At least 426 cases in Tanakh (adjacent, or separated only by לֹא/אַךְ/גַּם), 179 in the Torah; the infinitive after the verb is rare (31). In the Torah 36 stand in an אִם-condition (e.g. Ex 15:26; 19:5; 22:11, 12, 16, 22; 23:22; Num 21:2; Deut 11:13; 28:1). The Covenant Code alone has 27.
- **Often with acts that cannot be repeated:** אִם־גָּנֹב יִגָּנֵב (Ex 22:11); אִם־טָרֹף יִטָּרֵף (22:12); אִם־צָעֹק יִצְעַק (22:22); מוֹת יוּמָת.
- **With אמר**, 5 cases in Tanakh; none means "said twice":

  | Verse | Text | Sense |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ex 21:5 | אָמֹר יֹאמַר הָעֶבֶד | condition |
  | 1 Sam 20:21 | אִם־אָמֹר אֹמַר לַנַּעַר | condition, a one-time signal; the alternative signal in 20:22 has a plain verb, וְאִם־כֹּה אֹמַר לָעֶלֶם |
  | Judg 15:2 | אָמֹר אָמַרְתִּי | "I truly thought" |
  | 1 Sam 2:30 | אָמוֹר אָמַרְתִּי | "I did say" (GKC §113p) |
  | Ezek 28:9 | הֶאָמֹר תֹּאמַר | "will you still say?" |

- **GKC §113o** cites Ex 21:5 among conditions where the infinitive "emphasizes the importance of the condition on which some consequence depends." The sense of continuance belongs to the infinitive *after* the verb (§113r).
- **Deut 15:16 has a plain verb**, וְהָיָה כִּי־יֹאמַר אֵלֶיךָ, in a chapter that uses the doubled construction seven times elsewhere (15:4, 5, 8 ×2, 10, 11, 14). The Sifrei fills the gap from Exodus (121:1): יָכוֹל פַּעַם אַחַת? תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר "וְאִם אָמֹר יֹאמַר הָעֶבֶד", עַד שֶׁיֹּאמַר וְיִשְׁנֶה.
- **Translations.** Onkelos (מֵימַר יֵימַר) and Rasag (ואן קאל) are literal. Only Pseudo-Jonathan has a repetition, וְאִין יַתְנֵי וְיֵימַר עַבְדָא, writing the halacha into the translation.

**The tradition reads doubled verbs in four ways.**

| Reading | Where | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A count | MdRY Nezikin 2:10; Sifrei Devarim 121; Kiddushin 22a; Tractate Avadim 3:1; Y. Kiddushin 1:2; MdRShbY (three times) | אָמֹר יֹאמַר |
| "In any case" (מכל מקום) | Bava Metzia 31a–b; MdRY Nezikin 17:11 | הָשֵׁב תְּשִׁיבֵם, עָזֹב תַּעֲזֹב, פָּתֹחַ תִּפְתַּח, נָתוֹן תִּתֵּן |
| Continuation | Berakhot 40a | אִם שָׁמוֹעַ |
| Nothing derived: דִּבְּרָה תוֹרָה כִּלְשׁוֹן בְּנֵי אָדָם | R. Yishmael, Sanhedrin 64b (הִכָּרֵת תִּכָּרֵת); R. Elazar b. Azariah on הַעֲנֵיק תַּעֲנִיק in this same slave law (Bava Metzia 31b); R. Shimon on הַעֲבֵט תַּעֲבִיטֶנּוּ | |

At Bava Metzia 31a a scholar proposes the count reading to Rava three times (הָשֵׁב once, תְּשִׁיבֵם twice), and Rava rejects it each time: "הָשֵׁב" אֲפִילּוּ מֵאָה פְּעָמִים מַשְׁמַע.

**Malbim's rule** (*Ayelet HaShachar* §38): וכן כשכפל המקור על הפועל בא להורות שתעשה הפעולה בכל אופן ושיעשה אפילו כמה פעמים. All thirteen of its examples are Sifra derashot (אָשֹׁם אָשַׁם, הֵאָכֹל יֵאָכֵל, דָּרֹשׁ דָּרַשׁ…). It describes how the Sifra reads the construction, not Biblical usage; its "several times" half is what Rava rejects.

**אָהַבְתִּי.** A stative *qatal*, present in sense: "I love" (cf. Gen 27:4; Ps 116:1). Used of attachment across rank: a king to a retainer (1 Sam 16:21), a people to its leader (18:16), servants to David (18:22), treaty partners (1 Kgs 5:15). Deut 15:16 uses the same verb of the slave: כִּי אֲהֵבְךָ.

**אֶת־אִשְׁתִּי וְאֶת־בָּנָי.** In his own mouth, "*my* wife" and "*my* sons," where the ruling of v. 4 said הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ. Rashi: אִשְׁתִּי, the bondwoman. Midrash Tannaim on Deut 15:16: היה לעבד אשה ושפחה כנענית ולו ממנה בנים.

**הָעֶבֶד.** Anaphoric: the slave of v. 2. After v. 4's three subjects (אֲדֹנָיו; הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ; הוּא) a noun is needed to re-establish the speaker, and the code repeats subject nouns in consecutive sub-cases even without that need (Ex 22:6–7 הַגַּנָּב… הַגַּנָּב). It does not mark "while he is still a slave" (Sifrei 121:2; Kiddushin 22a): he is still עֶבֶד after the piercing (21:6; Deut 15:17 עֶבֶד עוֹלָם). Nor is it superfluous (Torah Temimah: המלה העבד מיותרת).

**חָפְשִׁי.** v. 2's promise, יֵצֵא לַחָפְשִׁי, becomes the first-person refusal לֹא אֵצֵא חָפְשִׁי. Exodus has לַחָפְשִׁי in the narrating voice (21:2, 26, 27) and bare חָפְשִׁי in the slave's speech.

**טוֹב לוֹ** (Deut 15:16, the source of two preconditions). טוֹב + ל + pronoun occurs 13 times (plain-text search) and always means being better off, never health: כִּי טוֹב לָנוּ עֲבֹד אֶת־מִצְרַיִם (Ex 14:12); Num 11:18; 14:3; Jer 22:15; כִּי טוֹב לִי אָז מֵעָתָּה (Hos 2:9). Deut 15:16 is the only one with עִם.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA):
1. [וְ] [אִם] [Modi אָמֹר] [Pred יֹאמַר] [Subj הָעֶבֶד]: the infinitive modifies the verb; it is not a second act of speaking
2. direct speech: [Pred אָהַבְתִּי] [Objc אֶת־אֲדֹנִי אֶת־אִשְׁתִּי וְאֶת־בָּנָי]
3. [Neg] [Pred אֵצֵא] [PrAd חָפְשִׁי]

**vv. 5–6 are one condition and its consequence.** The speech is the content of the condition; nothing in the syntax divides it into two utterances.

**The list is conjunctive.** About twenty lists of the shape אֶת X אֶת Y וְאֶת Z in BHSA; each is a plain list or an apposition (Deut 26:15), none a set of alternatives. The Covenant Code marks alternatives with אוֹ (26 times in 21:2–22:16). Cassuto's reading of the speech as alternatives, "I love my master" (if he was given no wife) *or* "my wife and my children," is not supported by the construction; Deut 15:16, where a slave with no master-given wife stays for love of master and household, gives the legal point a foothold.

**Deuteronomy's conditions are reasons, not words he must say.** Deut 15:16 quotes only לֹא אֵצֵא מֵעִמָּךְ. The following clauses are in the third person, the lawgiver's explanation of why such a slave stays: כִּי אֲהֵבְךָ וְאֶת־בֵּיתֶךָ כִּי־טוֹב לוֹ עִמָּךְ. Three rabbinic preconditions come from treating that motive as a condition: the master must have a wife and children (וְאֶת־בֵּיתֶךָ), must love the slave, and neither may be ill (כִּי־טוֹב לוֹ עִמָּךְ).

### Septuagint

*Brenton (1851); Rahlfs (1935) has the same wording.*

- **ἀποκριθεὶς εἴπῃ**: participle + verb, the LXX's usual rendering of the construction; at 1 Sam 20:21 it has ἐὰν εἴπω λέγων. No repetition.

- **dixerit**: a single verb, as at 1 Sam 20:21–22 (*si dixero… si autem sic locutus fuero*).

## Structure
- **The fourth sub-case of vv. 2–6**, and the only one that turns on the slave's choice: כִּי (v. 2); אִם / אִם (v. 3); אִם (v. 4); וְאִם (v. 5); consequence (v. 6).
- **The parallel law:**

  | | Ex 21:5–6 | Deut 15:16–17 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Condition | וְאִם אָמֹר יֹאמַר הָעֶבֶד | וְהָיָה כִּי־יֹאמַר אֵלֶיךָ |
  | Speech | אָהַבְתִּי אֶת־אֲדֹנִי אֶת־אִשְׁתִּי וְאֶת־בָּנָי | — |
  | Refusal | לֹא אֵצֵא חָפְשִׁי | לֹא אֵצֵא מֵעִמָּךְ |
  | Reason | — | כִּי אֲהֵבְךָ וְאֶת־בֵּיתֶךָ כִּי־טוֹב לוֹ עִמָּךְ (third person) |
  | Wife and children | given by the master (v. 4) | not mentioned |
  | The woman | separate law (v. 7) | וְאַף לַאֲמָתְךָ תַּעֲשֶׂה־כֵּן |

- **Deut 15:17b, "do likewise to your maidservant."** In Deuteronomy's laws כֵּן תַּעֲשֶׂה with ל otherwise always extends the immediately preceding instruction (20:15; 22:3 ×3; 12:4, 31); here that is the piercing. The Sifrei, Rashi and Rashbam (להענקה) refer it back to the gifts of 15:14. Ibn Ezra agrees and concedes the distance: דבק עם העניק תעניק לו ואם הוא רחוק. Bar Pedaya (Y. Kiddushin 1:2) reads it with its neighbour וְהָיָה לְךָ עֶבֶד עוֹלָם: like the pierced slave, the maidservant goes free at the master's death.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**He must say it twice.** MdRY Nezikin 2:10: שְׁנֵי פְעָמִים. MdRShbY 21:5: three times, עד שיאמר וישנה וישלש. Sifrei Devarim 121:1–2: once during the six years and once at the end, each tied to a phrase ("if the slave shall surely say" while he still serves; "I will not go out free" when he is entitled to leave). Followed by Chizkuni, Hoffmann (אמר וחזר ואמר), Hirsch ("repeatedly says") and Abarbanel (באמירות רבות).
- *Assessment.* A derashah, not the meaning of the construction (Words). The sources also disagree on how many and when: two or three declarations; during and end of the six (Sifrei), start and end (Kiddushin 22a), end of six and start of seven (Y. Kiddushin 1:2), only at the end (MdRShbY: הא אינו אומר אלא בסוף שש). The plain sense is one declaration at the time of release, closest to MdRShbY.
- Guggenheimer (on Y. Kiddushin 1:2) calls such count readings Akivan. Here the count is in MdRY, of R. Yishmael's school, and R. Akiva is the lenient party on the preconditions.

**The maidservant is not pierced.** From הָעֶבֶד (Sifrei; MdRShbY; Midrash Tannaim; Torah Temimah). *Assessment:* for Exodus it follows from the case itself, since the speaker has a wife (Rambam's argument, Avadim 3:13). The question is Deut 15:17b (Structure).

**The preconditions** (MdRY Nezikin 2:11–14; Sifrei Devarim 121:3–4; MdRShbY 21:5): the slave has a wife and children, and so does the master; each loves the other; neither is ill. MdRShbY names the tradents: R. Shimon's objection (if he did not love him, why would he stay?) and R. Elazar b. Azariah for health, from כִּי טוֹב לוֹ עִמָּךְ. It glosses the master's love: שנתברכו נכסים על ידיו.
- *Assessment.* Only the slave's wife and children are presupposed by Exodus's words. The master's family, mutual love and health are derived from Deuteronomy's statement of motive (Grammar), and טוֹב לוֹ never refers to health.
- **Dissent.** Tractate Avadim 3:1: R. Yishmael requires the family conditions on both sides; "R. ‘Aḳiba said: In either case he should have his ear pierced."
- **Approval.** Ibn Ezra's short commentary (quoted by Hoffmann): ומעתיקי התורה אמרו כי לא יירצע עבד עברי אם יחסר אחד מן התנאים… וכן דבר סורר ומורה, ודברם נכון.
- **Rationale.** Hirsch reads the set as a test of whether an irrevocable choice can be trusted: "If we imagine any one of these conditions to be lacking on either side, the decision may very well have been a too hasty one." A single master may later have a family, making "the conditions… intolerable"; an ailing slave may decide "out of a feeling of weakness and dependence which would pass when he is well again," an ailing master's slave "out of a feeling of self sacrifice"; one-sided affection is "indicative of an unnaturally emotional mood." Where both are ailing, Hirsch notes, the Talmud leaves the question undecided; on his analysis the two pressures may cancel. Rambam rules that he is not pierced (Avadim 3:11).

### Talmud
- **Kiddushin 22a.** Two declarations, at the start and end of the six years; the preconditions (wife and children on both sides, mutual love, health). Hirsch reports that the case of both ailing is left undecided.
- **Bava Metzia 31a–b.** Rava's rejection of count readings of doubled verbs; R. Elazar b. Azariah on הַעֲנֵיק תַּעֲנִיק (Deut 15:14), דִּבְּרָה תוֹרָה כִּלְשׁוֹן בְּנֵי אָדָם.
- **Y. Kiddushin 1:2.** Declarations at the end of six and start of seven; Bar Pedaya on Deut 15:17b.
- **The timing in practice.** Torah Temimah: the last declaration must come while a *perutah*'s worth of service remains, or it counts as said after the six years (Kiddushin 22a; the readings of Rashi, the Yerushalmi, Rambam and the Mishneh LaMelech on Avadim 3:10 diverge).

## Halacha
- **Rambam, Avadim 3:10.** עַד שֶׁיֹּאמַר וְיִשְׁנֶה… בְּסוֹף שֵׁשׁ בִּתְחִלַּת פְּרוּטָה אַחֲרוֹנָה. If he said it after the six years he is not pierced.
- **3:11.** Not pierced unless both slave and master have wife and children, each loves the other, and neither is ill: עַד שֶׁיִּהְיוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּטוֹבָה.
- **3:6.** The self-seller is not pierced; the court-sold slave who will not leave is pierced and serves to the Jubilee or the master's death.
- **3:13.** מִפִּי הַשְּׁמוּעָה לָמְדוּ שֶׁאֵין הָאִשָּׁה נִרְצַעַת וְכֵן יֵרָאֶה מֵעִנְיַן הַכָּתוּב, from אִשְׁתִּי; Deut 15:17b refers to the gifts.
- **1:9.** Equality in food, drink, clothing and lodging, from כִּי טוֹב לוֹ עִמָּךְ: מִכָּאן אָמְרוּ כָּל הַקּוֹנֶה עֶבֶד עִבְרִי כְּקוֹנֶה אָדוֹן לְעַצְמוֹ (Kiddushin 20a). Hirsch: "who buys a Jewish slave for himself has bought himself a master."
- **Sources**: MdRY Nezikin 2:10–14; Sifrei Devarim 121; MdRShbY 21:5; Kiddushin 22a. **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.
