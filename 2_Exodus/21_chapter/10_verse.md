# אִם־אַחֶ֖רֶת יִֽקַּֽח־ל֑וֹ שְׁאֵרָ֛הּ כְּסוּתָ֥הּ וְעֹנָתָ֖הּ לֹ֥א יִגְרָֽע׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Accents.** The atnach falls on ל֑וֹ (joined by maqaf to יִקַּח), dividing condition from consequence. No *setumah*: v. 11 continues the paragraph.
- **The accents do not group the three nouns.** שְׁאֵרָ֛הּ has tevir and כְּסוּתָ֥הּ merkha before the tipcha on וְעֹנָתָ֖הּ, the default chain for three words. Of 35 phrases of the shape "noun+suffix, noun+suffix, וְ+noun+suffix," 13 are accented exactly so, including the plain list גְּבִיעֶיהָ כַּפְתֹּרֶיהָ וּפְרָחֶיהָ (Ex 25:31 // 37:17).

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**שְׁאֵרָהּ.** שְׁאֵר occurs 16 times (Hebrew only), plus שַׁאֲרָה once (Lev 18:17):
- **Kin, 7: every other occurrence in the Torah** (Lev 18:6, 12, 13; 20:19; 21:2; 25:49; Num 27:11).
- **Flesh, body, 5** (Mic 3:2; Jer 51:35; Ps 73:26; Prov 5:11; 11:17).
- **Flesh eaten, meat, 3** (Ps 78:20, parallel to לֶחֶם; 78:27; Mic 3:3).

As food it is always meat, never food in general; that is Ramban's objection: why name food by meat rather than bread? "Wife" is not an attested sense; it is the Sifra's reading of Lev 21:2 (אֵין "שְׁאֵרוֹ" אֶלָּא אִשְׁתּוֹ), whose verse lists blood relatives without a wife. Conjugal relations is not attested either: in Lev 18:6 (Rebbi's proof text) the sexual sense is carried by לְגַלּוֹת עֶרְוָה, not by שְׁאֵר.

**Two nouns kept apart by the vowels.** שְׁאֵר ("flesh, kin") has ṣere in all 16 cases; שְׁאָר ("remnant") has qamats in all 26. Malbim's witness for "marital union," Mal 2:15 וּשְׁאָר רוּחַ לוֹ, and Ramban's for "near offspring," Isa 14:22 שֵׁם וּשְׁאָר נִין וָנֶכֶד, are both the other noun; Radak files both there.

**The earliest translators generalised to maintenance.** LXX τὰ δέοντα, "the necessities" (elsewhere it renders שְׁאֵר σάρξ or "kin"); Onkelos זִיוּנַהּ; Rasag טעאמהא; Menachem ibn Saruq (ענין מזון) and Radak (כל מזון סתם).

**כְּסוּתָהּ.** כְּסוּת (8) is a covering, in the ordinary case clothing. In the same code, Ex 22:26, it is the poor man's garment that serves as his blanket (כִּי הִוא כְסוּתֹה לְבַדָּהּ… בַּמֶּה יִשְׁכָּב). Ramban's "bed-covering" leans on that verse.

**עֹנָתָהּ.** Only here in Hebrew. Five proposed derivations:
1. **From ענה (piel), "afflict."** R. Yoshiyah derives conjugal rights from it (Gen 34:2 in MdRY Nezikin 3:23; Gen 31:50 in MdRShbY and Ketubot 47b); Rebbi derives food (Deut 8:3 וַיְעַנְּךָ וַיַּרְעִבֶךָ). Piel ענה with a woman as object (15) means forced or illegitimate intercourse in 13 cases and never names a husband's duty. Ibn Ezra on Gen 31:50 rejects "if you withhold relations from my daughters": וזאת לשון נכריה לא נשמע כמוה בכל המקרא. The nouns from this root are עֳנִי, עֱנוּת, תַּעֲנִית, never *ʿōnā*; Torah Temimah's point that "affliction" would be spelled וענותה therefore tells against both of the Mekhilta's derivations.
2. **From עֵת, "time," hence the times of relations.** Ibn Ezra; Radak (עת הקבוע לתשמיש); Rasag, ואוקאתהא, "her times." Mishnaic עוֹנָה means season or due time, and R. Yonathan uses it so in the same Mekhilta passage: נוֹתֵן לָהּ כָּל אֶחָד וְאֶחָד בְּעוֹנָתָהּ (MdRY Nezikin 3:24). Ibn Ezra cites Ezek 16:8, וְהִנֵּה עִתֵּךְ עֵת דֹּדִים. BDB favours the link.
3. **From עון, "dwell," hence housing.** Menachem ibn Saruq, as one option (מהפותרים אשר אומרים ענין שכינה ודירה); Rashbam; Bekhor Shor and Chizkuni (י"מ); Cassuto. מָעוֹן (19) is always a dwelling, but no form without the prefixed mem means "dwelling," and the verbs cited (Isa 13:22; Hos 2:17) are read "sing" or "raise the voice" by Ibn Ezra, Radak and BHSA.
4. **"Oil."** Eliyashiv Oren (*Tarbiz* 32, 1965) and Shalom Paul (*JNES* 28, 1969): Mesopotamian maintenance clauses list food, oil and clothing, and oil belongs to a wife's provision at Hos 2:7 and Ezek 16. No Hebrew word עֹנָה means oil; Paul takes an unknown root, Oren proposed emending to עִנֻּגָהּ. As reported by Lockshin; not read at first hand.
5. **"Upkeep" in general.** Jonathan Paradise (1997), repointing עֲנֻתָהּ. As reported by Lockshin.

*Assessment.* On Hebrew usage and the ancient translations: time, hence conjugal times > oil (strong outside evidence, none inside Hebrew) > housing (only מָעוֹן) > the derivations from "afflict."

**לֹא יִגְרָע.** גָּרַע occurs 22 times. All 11 other occurrences in the Torah reduce something already fixed: the brick quota (Ex 5:8, 11, 19), a valuation (Lev 27:18), a place at the Passover offering (Num 9:7), a name in the clan (Num 27:4), an inheritance (Num 36:3–4), the commandments (Deut 4:2; 13:1). Ezek 16:27, וָאֶגְרַע חֻקֵּךְ, is a husband cutting his wife's allowance with this verb (Rashi: מְזוֹנוֹתַיִךְ). "Withhold" would be מָנַע or חָשַׂךְ, both common and both used of food. So R. Yoshiyah's argument holds: the verse speaks of a woman who already has a portion. Onkelos (לָא יִתְמְנָע) and Rasag (לא ינקצהא) render every גרע in the Torah the same way, so they are no evidence here.

**אַחֶרֶת.** A bare feminine "another" as object, with no noun, occurs only here (elsewhere only 1 Sam 21:10, of a sword). The missing noun comes from the first woman's category: "another" presupposes that she is already a wife. Rashi: עָלֶיהָ; Mizrachi: אֵין עוֹנָה אֶלָּא בְּאִשְׁתּוֹ.

**Food and clothing as a pair.** Basic support is named by food and clothing alone six times (Gen 28:20; Deut 10:18; Isa 3:7; 4:1; Ezek 18:7, 16). Oil joins where a wife or the good life is in view (Hos 2:7; Ezek 16:9–19; Eccl 9:7–8). Isa 4:1: seven women offer לַחְמֵנוּ נֹאכֵל וְשִׂמְלָתֵנוּ נִלְבָּשׁ רַק יִקָּרֵא שִׁמְךָ עָלֵינוּ, waiving food and clothing and keeping the marriage itself. Radak there: מה שחייב אדם לעשות משלו שאר כסות. A third, marital term after food and clothing is natural; Isa 4:1 does not show that it is sexual relations specifically.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA): [אִם] [Objc אַחֶרֶת] [Pred יִקַּח] [Cmpl לוֹ]; [Objc שְׁאֵרָהּ כְּסוּתָהּ וְעֹנָתָהּ] [Neg] [Pred יִגְרָע]. The object stands before לֹא + *yiqtol*, the same frame as v. 11.

**Three coordinate items.** Of 35 phrases of this shape, 34 are plain lists; in one (Gen 26:28) the first noun is a heading. In none does one noun say how another is given, and in none does the last sum up the others. So neither R. Yonathan's and R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov's "clothing suited to her body and to the season" nor Paradise's "food and clothing, that is, upkeep" has support from the construction.

**Whose "her."** Malbim (*Ayelet HaShachar* §123) presumes that what follows attaches to the latter-named, אַחֶרֶת, and so reconstructs R. Yonathan's reading that v. 10 concerns the new wife. The rule fails for pronouns: in Lev 18:18, the one other law about a man and two women (וְאִשָּׁה אֶל־אֲחֹתָהּ לֹא תִקָּח לִצְרֹר לְגַלּוֹת עֶרְוָתָהּ עָלֶיהָ בְּחַיֶּיהָ), three "her"s in five words switch between the women with nothing to mark it. Sense decides, and in Ex 21:10 it points to the maidservant: every "her" in vv. 7–11 is hers, and לֹא יִגְרָע needs an existing portion.

**Who takes another: the master or the son.** v. 10 opens with bare אִם after v. 9's וְאִם, and in the Covenant Code bare אִם attaches at any level (21:30 narrows the case before it; 22:2b–3 returns past a sub-case; 22:7 is a parallel alternative). The syntax cannot decide. As Hoffmann says, it follows the ketiv/qere of v. 8: on the ketiv the master never designated her, so "another" is taken for the son; on the qere, which the evidence mildly favours, the master is the more natural subject. The Vulgate (*alteram ei acceperit*) has "another for him," the son; Shadal, Cassuto and Hoffmann on the qere take the master; Ibn Ezra (הוא או בנו), Bekhor Shor and Abarbanel leave both open.

**Translations.** Onkelos: זִיוּנַהּ כְּסוּתַהּ וְעוֹנָתַהּ, keeping the Aramaic cognate. Rasag: טעאמהא וכסותהא ואוקאתהא, "her food, clothing and times." Targum Jerusalem (above) and Pseudo-Jonathan: מְזוֹנָהּ, תַּכְשִׁיטָהּ ("her ornaments"), and for עֹנָה "his coming in to her" (Targum Jerusalem: מֵעַיְלָהּ וּמַפְקָהּ לְוָותָהּ). As reported by Lockshin: Neofiti ומעלה ומפקה לוותה; Peshitta ומשכבה, "her lying-with"; the Samaritan Targum עמירה כסואה ואתלבטתה, the third term "suffering," i.e. minimal support (Tal). Philo, reading the LXX, has necessaries, clothing and fellowship. No ancient translation renders שְׁאֵר "meat," and none has "oil" or "housing."

## Structure
- **The third sub-case** (אִם) of vv. 7–11.
- **Deut 21:10–17 is the closest legal parallel**, and Sifrei Devarim 213:8 already reads them together, glossing the captive's וְהָיְתָה לְךָ לְאִשָּׁה with שארה כסותה ועונתה לא יגרע.

  | Deut 21:14 (the captive wife) | Ex 21:8–11 (the maidservant) |
  |---|---|
  | וְשִׁלַּחְתָּהּ לְנַפְשָׁהּ | וְיָצְאָה (v. 11) |
  | וּמָכֹר לֹא־תִמְכְּרֶנָּה בַּכָּסֶף | לְמָכְרָהּ לֹא־יִמְשֹׁל (v. 8); אֵין כָּסֶף (v. 11) |
  | תַּחַת אֲשֶׁר עִנִּיתָהּ | בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ (v. 8) |

  The next law, Deut 21:15–17 (כִּי־תִהְיֶיןָ לְאִישׁ שְׁתֵּי נָשִׁים), protects the less-loved wife's firstborn, as Ex 21:10 protects the first wife when a second is taken. Deut 21:14 is the only place in Torah law where piel ענה is used of intercourse within a marriage, and there it names the wrong done to her.
- **Gen 31:50** joins the same two things in a warning to a husband: אִם־תְּעַנֶּה אֶת־בְּנֹתַי וְאִם־תִּקַּח נָשִׁים עַל־בְּנֹתַי. It is the best-chosen rabbinic proof text for עֹנָה, though what ענה means there is itself disputed (Yoma 77a and Rashi: withholding relations; Ibn Ezra: mistreatment; Radak: any deprivation).
- **Ezek 16 is the prophets' picture of a husband's provision**: עִתֵּךְ עֵת דֹּדִים and covering (16:8), washing and anointing with oil (16:9), clothing (16:10–13), flour, honey and oil to eat (16:13, 19), and the penalty וָאֶגְרַע חֻקֵּךְ (16:27). Hos 2:7, 10–11 is the same picture: bread, water, wool, flax, oil and drink.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**Three words, four readings.**

| | שְׁאֵרָהּ | כְּסוּתָהּ | עֹנָתָהּ |
|---|---|---|---|
| R. Yoshiyah (MdRY Nezikin 3:23; MdRShbY; Ketubot 47b) | food (Mic 3:3; Ps 78:27) | clothing | conjugal relations (Gen 34:2; 31:50) |
| R. Yonathan (MdRY Nezikin 3:24); R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov (Ketubot 48a) | all three about clothing: suited to her body and to the season | | |
| Rebbi / R. Elazar (MdRY Nezikin 3:25; Ketubot 47b) | conjugal relations (Lev 18:6) | clothing | food (Deut 8:3) |
| Ramban (noted by Hoffmann) | all three about conjugal relations: closeness of flesh, the bed-covering, the time | | |

*Assessment.* R. Yoshiyah's food rests on an attested sense, though the sense is meat; R. Yonathan's body rests on an attested sense; Rebbi's conjugal שְׁאֵר does not (Words). Malbim's account of the word as carrying food, kinship and marital union holds for two of the three. Malbim also explains the tannaim's different assignments by who the verse is about and his rule that the obvious item comes first (*Ayelet HaShachar* §199): for R. Yoshiyah the maidservant, whose food is obvious; for Rebbi a free wife, whose conjugal right is obvious.

**Which woman.** R. Yoshiyah: the maidservant, from לֹא יִגְרָע (MdRY Nezikin 3:26: מִמִּי גּוֹרְעִים? מִמִּי שֶׁכְּבַר נָתְנוּ לוֹ); otherwise Scripture would have said לא ימנע or לא יחשך. R. Yonathan: the new, free wife. Hoffmann: both are students of R. Yishmael and read the ketiv, on which the master never designated her and v. 10 concerns the son, making one of vv. 9–10 redundant; that redundancy is what they dispute. On R. Akiva's qere the problem does not arise. *Assessment:* R. Yoshiyah's argument holds (Words).

**The onah schedule.** MdRShbY 21:10: איזו היא עונתה? כל אחד ואחד לפי מלאכתו. הטיילים בכל לילה; בעלי אומנות פעמים בשבת; החמרים פעם אחת בשבת; הגמלים פעם בחדש; הספנין פעם לששה חדשים; תלמידי חכמים מלילי שבת ללילי שבת. Shadal objects to Radak's fixed-schedule reading (דבר שאין הדעת סובלתו: the Torah has no such calendar), rejects R. Yoshiyah's derivation as רחוק מאד, and reaches conjugal relations by his own etymology.

**A wife rises with her husband.** MdRShbY from לֹא יִגְרָע: האשה עולה עם בעלה ואינה יורדת (also Ketubot 48a, 61a). *Assessment:* derash; the verb presupposes a baseline but does not say whose.

**A man must marry off his young son.** MdRY Nezikin 3:22, from אִם־אַחֶרֶת יִקַּח־לוֹ read of the son, with Deut 4:9. Torah Temimah and Malbim reconstruct the argument: for an adult son the new wife's needs would fall on him and there would be nothing for the master to diminish, so the verse must concern a minor son supported by his father. Against Sanhedrin 76b (the danger of marrying children off close to maturity), Malbim reads "minor" as a thirteen-year-old still at his father's table (Bava Metzia 12b).

**Why the Torah states a wife's rights here.** Hirsch: "This is the only place where the Torah speaks of the duties of a man towards his wife. When it wants to lay down the משפט בנות, the elementary rights of the daughters of its people, it picks out for its example a woman of the very lowest social grade." Set beside a bride of a free family, "Not by one hairbreadth may the treatment of the one differ from that given to the other."

### Talmud
- **Ketubot 47b–48a.** The dispute of R. Yoshiyah and R. Yonathan, the latter under the name of R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov. Rava cites the baraita to show that maintenance is a Torah obligation. Rav Yosef: שְׁאֵרָהּ is closeness of flesh, not cohabiting clothed as the Persians do, supporting Rav Huna that a man who insists on it must divorce and pay the ketubah.
- **Whether maintenance is Torah law.** Malbim notes that Ketubot 47b gives R. Yonathan's view as R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov's, without the *a fortiori* from maidservant to wife, and on that view a wife's maintenance is rabbinic (so too SMaG and Sefer Yere'im); the *a fortiori* is then an asmachta. Hirsch lists Ramban, the Maggid Mishneh and the Ran as holding maintenance rabbinic; Abarbanel: כי המזון הוא מתקנת חז"ל ואינו מן התורה. Hirsch turns this into an argument: "The legal claim of children to be fed is also only תקנת חכמים!", and the Torah entrusts both to natural feeling.
- **Ketubot 61b; Mishnah Ketubot 5:6.** The onah schedule by occupation.
- **Yoma 77a.** Gen 31:50's אִם־תְּעַנֶּה as withholding relations.
- **Yevamot 22b; Sifra, Emor 1:4.** שְׁאֵרוֹ, his wife.
- **Nedarim 15b.** A vow to withhold relations falls under "he shall not profane his word," because he is already bound by שארה כסותה ועונתה לא יגרע.

## Halacha
- **Mishnah Ketubot 5:6**: הָעוֹנָה הָאֲמוּרָה בַתּוֹרָה, הַטַּיָּלִין, בְּכָל יוֹם. הַפּוֹעֲלִים, שְׁתַּיִם בַּשַּׁבָּת. הַחַמָּרִים, אַחַת בַּשַּׁבָּת. הַגַּמָּלִים, אַחַת לִשְׁלֹשִׁים יוֹם. הַסַּפָּנִים, אַחַת לְשִׁשָּׁה חֳדָשִׁים, דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר.
- **Rambam, Ishut 12:1–2.** A husband has ten obligations to his wife; וְהָעֲשָׂרָה שְׁלֹשָׁה מֵהֶן מִן הַתּוֹרָה… שְׁאֵרָהּ אֵלּוּ מְזוֹנוֹתֶיהָ. כְּסוּתָהּ כְּמַשְׁמָעוֹ. עוֹנָתָהּ לָבֹא עָלֶיהָ כְּדֶרֶךְ כָּל הָאָרֶץ; the other seven are rabbinic. Rambam follows R. Yoshiyah.
- **Shulchan Arukh, Even HaEzer 69:1–2.** The ten obligations, including food, clothing and onah.
- **EH 76:11.** אסור לאדם למנוע מאשתו עונתה ואם מנעה כדי לצערה עובר בלא תעשה דעונתה לא יגרע.
- **EH 77:1.** A husband who refuses relations while maintaining her עובר בל"ת שנא' לא יגרע (with a gloss limiting this if he divorces at once).
- The verse about a purchased maidservant thus became the Torah source for every wife's rights. Shadal states the *a fortiori*: if the Torah requires this for a maidservant whose father sold her in poverty, how much more for a free wife.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Cassuto**: שארה הוא כנראה ביטוי עתיק למזונות, קשור במנהג הקדמון של הנוודים, שעיקר מחייתם בבשר (Arabic *laḥm*, meat); עונתה is her housing, כך נראה לפרש עונתה לפי הפשט, יותר מאשר לפי המסורת המאוחרת: זמני הביאה.
- **Sarna** (*Exodus*, JPS, 1991, 121; as reported by Lockshin): the conjugal reading "has no philological support"; the oil reading is "persuasive, although as yet philologically unsustained."
- **Lockshin** (TheTorah.com) surveys the readings and the translations; Deem (*JSS* 23, 1978) and Morag (*Tarbiz* 68, 1998) argue for a sexual sense of ענה at Hos 2:17, which would supply the Hebrew parallel the conjugal reading lacks. Not read at first hand.

### Ancient Near Eastern Context
Maintenance clauses listing food, oil and clothing (as reported by Lockshin, citing Paul):
- **Lipit-Ishtar §27**: "he shall provide grain, oil, and clothing rations for the prostitute."
- **Eshnunna §32**: food, oil and clothing rations owed for three years to a caregiver.
- **Ptahhotep §21** (Sarna): "fill her belly, clothe her back, ointment soothes her body."
