# וְכִֽי־יִמְכֹּ֥ר אִ֛ישׁ אֶת־בִּתּ֖וֹ לְאָמָ֑ה לֹ֥א תֵצֵ֖א כְּצֵ֥את הָעֲבָדִֽים׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Paragraphing.** This verse opens the second paragraph (vv. 7–11), after the *setumah* that ends v. 6.
- **Accents.** The atnach on לְאָמָ֑ה divides case from rule, the ordinary division of a short verse.
- **כְּצֵאת** occurs 3 times (Ex 21:7; 33:8; Judg 5:31), as the Masoretic note cited by Ba'al HaTurim says (כצאת ג' במסור').

### Words
**וְכִי יִמְכֹּר אִישׁ.**
- **The form.** The case law of 21:2–22:16 has 17 conditions opened by כִּי; 11 have the *vav* (21:7, 14, 18, 20, 22, 26, 28, 33, 35; 22:13, 15). All are third person except the first, 21:2 כִּי תִקְנֶה. v. 7's "if a man sells" is the code's normal form; v. 2 is the exception.
- **Casuistic אִישׁ.** The same formula (וְכִי + verb + אִישׁ) opens 21:14, 20, 26, 33; 22:13, 15. The Mekhilta reads it as including women at 21:20 and 21:26 (Nezikin 7:2, 9:2: לעשות בהן נשין כאנשין) and, through 21:29, at 21:33. The school of R. Elazar reads 21:1 as השוה הכתוב אשה לאיש לכל דינין שבתורה (Bava Kamma 15a). At 21:7 the Mekhilta reads it as excluding women (Nezikin 3:2, 3:4). The LXX renders all of them τις, "anyone."
- **Where the code means both sexes it usually names both**: אִישׁ אוֹ אִשָּׁה (21:28, 29); בֵן… בַת (21:31); עַבְדּוֹ אוֹ אֲמָתוֹ (21:20, 26, 27).
- **Every Torah law on a daughter's marriage gives it to her father**: אִם־מָאֵן יְמָאֵן אָבִיהָ (Ex 22:16); her vows, בֵּין־אָב לְבִתּוֹ (Num 30:4–6, 17); אֶת־בִּתִּי נָתַתִּי (Deut 22:16), the verse Sotah 23b cites. At Nuzi, mothers and brothers also gave daughters in marriage (Justel, below).

**אֶת־בִּתּוֹ.**
- **בַּת names a relation, not an age**: Rachel of marriageable age is בַּת־לָבָן (Gen 29:10); Saul offers בִּתִּי הַגְּדוֹלָה (1 Sam 18:17).
- **When the Torah limits a father's power by his daughter's age, it says so**: בֵּין־אָב לְבִתּוֹ בִּנְעֻרֶיהָ בֵּית אָבִיהָ (Num 30:17; also 30:4; Lev 22:13). Ex 21:7 has no such phrase.
- **Sons** were also taken or sold for debt: לָקַחַת אֶת־שְׁנֵי יְלָדַי לוֹ לַעֲבָדִים (2 Kgs 4:1); כֹבְשִׁים אֶת־בָּנֵינוּ וְאֶת־בְּנֹתֵינוּ לַעֲבָדִים (Neh 5:5); Isa 50:1; Hammurabi §117. Hoffmann concedes that sons were sold in practice.

**לְאָמָה.**
- **The ל names the capacity.** The Torah's laws sell persons at Ex 21:16; 22:2; Lev 25:39, 47; Deut 15:12; 21:14; 24:7, and none names a status (every מכר in the Torah checked). Ex 21:7 is the only one that does, as Torah Temimah and Malbim observe. Elsewhere the status is named when it matters: לְעֶבֶד נִמְכַּר יוֹסֵף (Ps 105:17); לַעֲבָדִים וְלִשְׁפָחוֹת (Deut 28:68).
- **A daughter could be "sold" in another capacity**: Rachel and Leah say of their father כִּי מְכָרָנוּ (Gen 31:15). The formula for giving a daughter in marriage is נָתַן אֶת־בִּתּוֹ… לְאִשָּׁה (15 clauses with a suffixed בַּת, e.g. Deut 22:16; Josh 15:16–17; 1 Sam 18:27). לְאָמָה says which transaction this is, so it is not superfluous (against Kiddushin 19b and Ketubot 46b–47a, Talmud below).

**אָמָה and שִׁפְחָה.**
- אָמָה 56 times, שִׁפְחָה 63 times. The laws use אָמָה (19 times in Exodus–Deuteronomy); שִׁפְחָה only at Lev 19:20 and in the curse of Deut 28:68.
- **The foreign bondwoman is an אָמָה too** (Lev 25:44), and **Jeremiah calls the Hebrew bondwoman a שִׁפְחָה** six times (34:9, 10, 11 ×2, 16 ×2). The rabbinic pair אָמָה עִבְרִיָּה / שִׁפְחָה כְּנַעֲנִית is not biblical usage.
- **In narrative the words overlap for the same woman**: Hagar (שִׁפְחָה Gen 16:1–8; אָמָה 21:10–13); Bilhah (29:29; 30:3); Ruth (2:13; 3:9); Abigail (1 Sam 25:24–31, 41). שִׁפְחָה is the word for menial work (Ex 11:5; הִנֵּה אֲמָתְךָ לְשִׁפְחָה לִרְחֹץ רַגְלֵי, 1 Sam 25:41), which is Hoffmann's evidence for a difference of rank. BDB lists אָמָה "applied to concubine," including Ex 21:7.

**לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת.**
- **In Exodus 21, יָצָא is the release of vv. 2–5 and of v. 11; the tooth-and-eye release uses another verb.**

  | Release | Verb | Where |
  |---|---|---|
  | The bondman's, and hers | יָצָא (qal) | Ex 21:2, 3 ×2, 4, 5; 21:7 ×2; 21:11; Lev 25:41, 54; Deut 15:16 |
  | Tooth and eye | שִׁלַּח לַחָפְשִׁי (piel) | Ex 21:26, 27 |
  | Seventh year in Deuteronomy and Jeremiah | שִׁלַּח חָפְשִׁי (piel) | Deut 15:12, 13, 18; Jer 34:9–16 |

- **Rabbinic Hebrew uses יָצָא for every release**, the injury release included: לֹא תֵצֵא בְרָאשֵׁי אֵבָרִים כְּדֶרֶךְ שֶׁהַכְּנַעֲנִים יוֹצְאִים (MdRY Nezikin 3:9). Read in that idiom, v. 7 can point to vv. 26–27; in the idiom of Exodus it points to vv. 2–5.

**הָעֲבָדִים.**
- The plural with the article occurs 3 times: Ex 21:7; Jer 34:11 (וַיָּשִׁבוּ אֶת־הָעֲבָדִים… אֲשֶׁר שִׁלְּחוּ חָפְשִׁים, the freed Hebrews); Joel 3:2.
- **Hebrew slaves are called plain עֶבֶד**: הָעֶבֶד (Ex 21:5); עֶבֶד עוֹלָם (Deut 15:17); Jer 34:11, 16; 2 Kgs 4:1; Neh 5:5. Only Lev 25 keeps the word away from Israelites (25:39, 42). The claim that a Hebrew slave is never called plain עֶבֶד, so that הָעֲבָדִים must be Canaanites (Ramban at 21:7; Rabbeinu Bahya; Or HaChaim; Hirsch), fails.
- With the article the nearest referent is the slave of the paragraph just ended. So Ramban's plain-sense reading, in his comment on 21:8: ודרך הפשט בפרשה זו שיאמר כי ימכר איש את בתו הקטנה לאמה לא תצא כצאת העבדים האלה הנזכרים בשביעית (שמות כ"א:ב') וביובל.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA):
1. [Conj וְ] [Conj כִּי] [Pred יִמְכֹּר] [Subj אִישׁ] [Objc אֶת־בִּתּוֹ] [Adju לְאָמָה]
2. [Nega לֹא] [Pred תֵצֵא]
3. infinitive clause: [Pred כְּצֵאת] [Subj הָעֲבָדִים]

**A negative rule, framed by v. 11.** What she does receive is set out in vv. 8–11, and the last sub-case uses the same verb positively: וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם אֵין כָּסֶף (v. 11). So לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת denies the manner of her going out, not that she goes out. Which of the bondman's rules it sets aside (the six-year term, the terms about wife and children, or all of them) the clause alone does not say.

**"To V as Y's V-ing"** (כְּ + infinitive construct of the same verb) is an ordinary comparison of manner, in 25 other verses (BHSA verse search, classified by reading): וַיְשַׁסְּעֵהוּ כְּשַׁסַּע הַגְּדִי (Judg 14:6); כִּנְפוֹל לִפְנֵי בְנֵי־עַוְלָה נָפָלְתָּ (2 Sam 3:34); negated, with a different subject, וְלֹא נִכְנַע… כְּהִכָּנַע מְנַשֶּׁה אָבִיו (2 Chr 33:23). Rav Chisda's inference that כְּצֵאת is extra (the verse could have said כָּעֲבָדִים) fails.

**Translations.** Onkelos (לָא תִפּוֹק כְּמַפְקָנוּת עַבְדַיָּא) and Rasag (פלא תכ̇רג̇ כאלעביד) follow the Hebrew, and both keep the two release verbs apart (vv. 26–27: יְפַטְּרִנֵּהּ; פליטלקה). Pseudo-Jonathan writes the halacha into the verse: יַת בְּרַתֵּיהּ זְעֵירְתָּא ("his little daughter"); כְּנַעֲנָאֵי דְּמִשְׁתַּחְרְרִין בְּשִׁינָא וְעֵינָא אֱלָהֵן בִּשְׁנִין דִּשְׁמִיטָתָא וּבְסִימָנַיָא וּבְיוּבְלָא וּבְמוֹתַת רִבּוֹנָהָא וּפוּרְעַן כַּסְפָּא. Even there the injury release has a different verb.

### Septuagint
Ἐὰν δέ τις ἀποδῶται τὴν ἑαυτοῦ θυγατέρα οἰκέτιν, οὐκ ἀπελεύσεται, ὥσπερ ἀποτρέχουσιν αἱ δοῦλαι.

*Brenton (1851); Rahlfs (1935) has the same wording.*

- **τις**: "anyone."
- **οἰκέτιν**: "household servant," a different noun from the παιδίσκη (vv. 20, 32) and θεράπαινα (vv. 26–27) used for the אָמָה elsewhere in the chapter.
- **αἱ δοῦλαι**: "as the *female* slaves depart." She is compared with other bondwomen, not with the bondman. Hoffmann (n. 70) notes the Vulgate's feminine and suggests the translators meant that the father-sold girl does not go out like a woman sold otherwise. The Göttingen apparatus has not been checked.
- **Release verbs.** ἀπελεύσεται for her and for the bondman (vv. 2–5); ἐξαποστελεῖ, "he shall send away," for vv. 26–27.

### Vulgate
Si quis vendiderit filiam suam in famulam, non egredietur sicut ancillae exire consueverunt.

*Clementine Vulgate.*

- **in famulam**: a different noun from the *ancilla* of vv. 20, 26–27, 32.
- **sicut ancillae exire consueverunt**: "as maidservants are accustomed to go out," feminine, as in the LXX.
- **Release verbs.** *egredietur* here; *dimittet* for vv. 26–27.

## Structure
- **The two paragraphs are built alike**: one כִּי case and four אִם sub-cases each.

  | | vv. 2–6: the bondman | vv. 7–11: the daughter |
  |---|---|---|
  | Case | כִּי תִקְנֶה עֶבֶד עִבְרִי: "you" buy | וְכִי־יִמְכֹּר אִישׁ אֶת־בִּתּוֹ לְאָמָה: "a man" sells |
  | Rule | שֵׁשׁ שָׁנִים יַעֲבֹד וּבַשְּׁבִעִת יֵצֵא לַחָפְשִׁי חִנָּם | לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת הָעֲבָדִים |
  | Sub-cases | אִם (3a), אִם (3b), אִם (4), וְאִם (5) | אִם (8), וְאִם (9), אִם (10), וְאִם (11) |
  | Subject | his wife and children at release; his refusal to leave | designation and redemption; designation to a son; another wife; failure |
  | Last words | וַעֲבָדוֹ לְעֹלָם | וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם אֵין כָּסֶף |

- **v. 2 and v. 11** are the only uses of חִנָּם in the unit, both with יָצָא.
- **vv. 26–27 come nineteen verses later**, in the laws of injury. Abarbanel: עדין לא צוה בזה.
- **The conflict is with Deuteronomy**, which puts the woman under the six-year release (Deut 15:12, 17). Every reading of v. 7 is also a position on the two laws: the Mekhilta harmonises by sending v. 7 forward to vv. 26–27; Shadal and Koller take the laws to concern different women; the view Farber reports takes Deuteronomy to replace Exodus.
- **The descending sequence.** R. Yose b. R. Chanina (Arakhin 30b; Kiddushin 20a) places the sale of a daughter in a sequence of ruin from Lev 25: movables, fields, house, borrowing at interest, his daughter, himself. The Gemara notes that the daughter is not in that passage: ואף על גב דבתו בהאי ענינא ליתה. See [21:2](02_verse.md), Cross-References.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**The restrictions** (MdRY Nezikin 3:1–8; MdRShbY 21:7).

| Restriction | Derivation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Only a minor | analogy to the annulment of vows (Num 30), then narrowed by the release at puberty (v. 11); MdRShbY: בתו בת ולא אם | Not in the words (see Words). Hoffmann: והנה אי אפשר להוכיח הלכה זו מדברי הכתוב. ברם, סברה היא: the Torah gave the father no rights over his son, so presumably over his daughter only while a minor. The step from *na'arah* to minor rests on reading v. 11 as puberty. |
| The father, not the mother | אִישׁ (also Mishnah Sotah 3:8; Sotah 23b); Lekach Tov: האיש מוכר את בתו, ולא האשה מוכרת את בתה | The grammar allows it but does not require it (the Mekhilta's own reading of אִישׁ at 21:20, 26, 33). Well supported by the Torah's family law (Words). |
| A daughter, not a son | בִּתּוֹ, blocking the *a fortiori* from the son, who can be sold by the court | An argument from silence; sons were sold in practice. |
| She cannot sell herself | the verse frames the sale as the father's act; Malbim: it could have said כִּי תִקְנֶה אָמָה | Not evidence: the third person is the code's norm and v. 2 the exception. Ramban (as reported by Hoffmann) and Shadal hold that a woman may sell herself (Deut 15:12's niphal). |
| Not sold for her own theft | MdRShbY, from 21:37's כִּי יִגְנֹב אִישׁ: האיש נמכר בגניבה ואין האשה נמכרת בגניבתה; nor for her father's | Legal reasoning; MdRY on 21:7 adds that she is not sold because she is not pierced. |
| Only to one who could marry her | MdRShbY: וכשהוא מוכרה אינו מוכרה אלא למי שיש לו בה קידושין | Follows from vv. 8–9. |
| Only in extreme poverty | MdRShbY: שאין אדם רשאי למכור את בתו אלא אם כן העני ולא מצא מה יאכל; Tosefta Arakhin 5:3, for field, daughter and self alike | Legal policy, not a reading. |
| Not sold twice | לְאָמָה is singular (R. Yose HaGelili, MdRY Nezikin 3:8); Ramban and Kiddushin 18a from בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ | The singular is not evidence. |

Malbim shows how the restrictions depend on each other, and that several depend on reading v. 11 as her release at puberty, the derivation ranked weakest in the unit (see [21:11](11_verse.md)).

**Why the Torah allows the sale at all.** Abarbanel: כי על הרוב לא יקנה אדם נערה קטנה לעבוד עבודה כי היא לא תוכל לעבוד לחולשת שכלה ואיבריה אלא שישאנה ליופיה או לטוב מעשיה כדי ליעדה לו או לבנו ונתן כספו על זה. כי כן היה דרכם לתת בעד הבתולות מוהר ומתן ועל מנת כך מכרה אביה לא בעבור שגנבה ולא לאכול ולשתות הוא את כספה. Hirsch reaches the same from the age limit: "We can already see from this limitation that the intended eventuality of marriage is the predominant idea of the 'sale'." He adds Kiddushin 41a (אסור לאדם שיקדש את בתו כשהיא קטנה עד שתגדל ותאמר בפלוני אני רוצה): "This not only declares any forced marriage as sinful, but even the only too common practice of persuading a daughter into a marriage as equally so." And v. 8 calls the father's act בגידה, which "shows, clearly enough, that the case is to be regarded only as an exceptional one."

**"She shall not go out as the slaves go out": the readings.**
1. **Not freed for tooth or eye** (vv. 26–27). R. Akiva (MdRShbY 21:7), against R. Eliezer; MdRY Nezikin 3:9; Rav Huna (Kiddushin 16a); Rashi; Rambam, Avadim 4:6. The Mekhilta itself raises "not at the years and the Jubilee" and rejects it only from Deut 15:12. Abarbanel objects: אינו נכון, כי עדין לא צוה בזה, והעבריה מה לה עם הכנעניים? Ramban at 21:7 asks why the verse would be needed: וַאֲנִי תָּמֵהַּ, וְלָמָּה הֻצְרַךְ הַכָּתוּב לוֹמַר כֵּן. Hirsch defends it on moral grounds: freedom for an injury would be "no equivalent for bodily injuries"; she receives full damages and a Jewish girl "would expect, without consideration of the crippling she has suffered, or just because of it with greater confidence, that the master would marry her."
2. **Not after six years; she stays to be married.** Rashbam; Ramban at 21:8 (ודרך הפשט); Or HaChaim (second explanation); the Gra; Hoffmann (the plain sense); Cassuto: חוק זה נקבע לטובתה של הנערה… התורה חסה עליה והעניקה לה זכות חשובה יותר מזכות היציאה לחירות אחר זמן מסויים. The Netziv: לפי הפשט משמעו שאינה יוצאת בשום יציאת עבד… משום שמצוה ליעדה… והיא כעין פילגש (with Rambam, Melakhim 4:4); she leaves only by a *get*.
3. **Not on the marital terms of vv. 3–4** (her husband going out with her; a partner given by the master). Abarbanel.
4. **Not without her children**: if he wants children by her he must marry her. Bekhor Shor; Chizkuni (דבר אחר).
5. **She goes free at the master's death**, which the bondman does not. Or HaChaim (first explanation).
6. **Earlier, at puberty.** Shadal; Reggio.
7. **A restriction on her work.** R. Eliezer (MdRShbY): שלא תהא נוטלת אחריו דלאים ובלריות למרחץ; R. Akiva answers that Lev 25:39 already forbids it. R. Yaakov b. Asher, cited in the responsa of Maharai of Brünn §241 (via Torah Temimah): he may not send her out to work, only in the house; applied there to an actual dispute. Chizkuni, Hadar Zekenim and Tur HaArokh give it as the plain sense; Da'at Zekenim attributes it to Ibn Ezra, but neither of Ibn Ezra's commentaries has it (the short one: ואין משפט יציאתה לחופש כזכרים).

*Assessment.* Reading 2 fits the words best: the verb and the article point back to vv. 2–5, and v. 11 supplies her own release. Readings 3–5 are allowed and anchored in vv. 3–4 but not required. Reading 1, the halacha, is a harmonisation of Exodus with Deuteronomy in rabbinic idiom, not the sense of Ex 21:7. The halacha agrees with reading 2 once she is designated (Rambam, Avadim 4:7). Reading 7 is not the sense here, since יָצָא in both paragraphs is release.

### Talmud
- **Kiddushin 16a.** Rav Huna: from v. 7, she is not freed for tooth or eye. Rav Chisda: כְּצֵאת is extra and teaches a second point (fails; see Grammar). Rav Acha bar Yaakov: she does not go out as slaves do, but is acquired as they are, by document. The Gemara itself asks which half of the comparison to apply (וּמָה רָאִיתָ). An inference from contrast.
- **Kiddushin 18a.** The Sifrei (Devarim 118:5), which seems to deny her release by years, Jubilee and deduction, speaks of a maidservant already designated (כגון שיעדה): designated, she is a wife and leaves only by a *get*. Hoffmann finds the derivation תמוה but agrees that the plain sense is Rashbam's and Ramban's.
- **Kiddushin 18b.** Whether a father may sell her into servitude after servitude: R. Yochanan concedes that the sources reflect different tannaim (permitted in Tractate Avadim 1:4; forbidden in MdRY Nezikin 3:8).
- **Kiddushin 19b; Ketubot 46b–47a.** לְאָמָה as superfluous, teaching: sale to men she may not marry (the Rabbis), to relatives (R. Eliezer), a valid stipulation against designation (for R. Meir), and Rav Huna in Rav's name, that a daughter's earnings belong to her father, as a maidservant's to her master. The Gemara asks why this is not drawn from Num 30:17.
- **Kiddushin 20a.** R. Yose b. R. Chanina: מוטב שימכור אדם את בתו ולא ילוה ברבית. Rashi: she can deduct her redemption and leave. Torah Temimah: her term diminishes of itself while a debt at interest grows; the two are exact opposites.
- **Sotah 23b.** האיש מוכר את בתו ואין האשה מוכרת את בתה; Torah Temimah's reason: the father's right to annul her vows and to her earnings carries the right to sell her.
- **Kiddushin 41a.** A father may not betroth his minor daughter until she is grown and says whom she wants (Hirsch's use above).

## Halacha
- **Mishnah Kiddushin 1:2**: יְתֵרָה עָלָיו אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה, שֶׁקּוֹנָה אֶת עַצְמָהּ בְּסִימָנִין. **Mishnah Sotah 3:8**: the father sells his daughter; the mother does not.
- **Rambam, Avadim 4:1.** אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה הִיא הַקְּטַנָּה שֶׁמְּכָרָהּ אָבִיהָ; once she shows signs of puberty he may not sell her.
- **4:2.** אֵין הָאָב רַשַּׁאי לִמְכֹּר אֶת בִּתּוֹ אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן הֶעֱנִי וְלֹא נִשְׁאַר לוֹ כְּלוּם לֹא קַרְקַע וְלֹא מִטַּלְטְלִין וַאֲפִלּוּ כְּסוּת שֶׁעָלָיו; and the father is compelled to redeem her afterwards, because of the family's dishonour.
- **4:3.** Acquired by money or document, not for a *perutah*, since the sum must allow deduction.
- **4:4–5.** She serves six years like the court-sold slave (from Deut 15:12), goes free at the Jubilee, at the master's death even if he left a son, by deduction or writ, and in addition at puberty.
- **4:6.** אֵין אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה יוֹצְאָה בְּרָאשֵׁי אֵיבָרִים שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שמות כא ז) "לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת הָעֲבָדִים"; the master pays damages, and likewise for a Hebrew bondman. Six modes of release in all.
- **4:7.** Once designated to the master or his son she is like any betrothed woman and leaves only by death or a *get*.
- **4:11–14.** Sold only to one whom she or his son could marry; not into servitude after marriage; but again into servitude after servitude, while a minor.
- **Hilkhot Melakhim 4:4** (cited by the Netziv): an ordinary man may not take a concubine, except a Hebrew maidservant after designation.
- **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Complementary laws.** Koller (TheTorah.com): Exodus treats "a very particular type of a female slave: the dependent daughter who is sold by her father for purposes of marriage," and Deuteronomy fills the gap for "a woman who was sold into slavery because of an outstanding debt she is unable to pay, as a man is." Shadal makes the same division (Deut 15:12 speaks of an adult who sold herself); so, as reported in *The Torah: A Women's Commentary*, does Chirichigno.
- **Replacement.** Farber reports that "several scholars suggest that Deuteronomy improved the plight of women, and thus is abolishing the law of Exodus 21:7-11," treating the female slave on a par with the male.
- **A disguised marriage?** Justel, "Exodus 21, the Sale of Daughters, and the Cuneiform Sources of Nuzi," *VT* 72 (2022) 431–443 (open access; read): the verb is מָכַר and the status אָמָה, where Hebrew "takes" or "gives" a wife; the transaction is a sale into service aimed at marriage. Fleishman (*Father-Daughter Relations in Biblical Law*, 2011) and Westbrook are known here only through Justel.

### Ancient Near Eastern Context
- **Nuzi** (Justel, read first-hand). Daughters were handed over by mothers (AASOR 16 30; HSS 19 117) and brothers (AASOR 16 23), not only fathers. HSS 19 117 has a mother give her daughter to one man "as *amtu*" and "as wife." Mendelsohn's Nuzi parallels are matrimonial adoptions, not sales.
- **Old Assyrian *amtum*** as a secondary wife (Kienast, *AoF* 35, 2008; as reported by Justel).
- **Hammurabi §117.** A debtor's wife, son or daughter sold for debt serves three years and goes free in the fourth (King's 1910 translation). Cassuto: under Hammurabi such a girl would be freed after three years; the Torah gives her a more valuable right, to remain as a wife.
