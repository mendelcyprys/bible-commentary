# אִם־בְּגַפּ֥וֹ יָבֹ֖א בְּגַפּ֣וֹ יֵצֵ֑א אִם־בַּ֤עַל אִשָּׁה֙ ה֔וּא וְיָצְאָ֥ה אִשְׁתּ֖וֹ עִמּֽוֹ׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Accents.** The atnach on יֵצֵ֑א divides two complete cases, each with its own condition and outcome (in vv. 7–9 the atnach divides case from rule). The zaqef qatan on ה֔וּא ends the second condition. בְּגַפּוֹ is joined to its verb by a conjunctive accent in both halves (merkha, munach).

### Words
**בְּגַפּוֹ.** "Alone, without wife or children."
- **Occurrences.** Three, all in this law: 21:3 (twice) and 21:4. BHSA and BDB treat גַּפֵּי in Prov 9:3 (עַל־גַּפֵּי מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת) as a different word, "height."
- **The sense is fixed by the opposites.** In v. 3 the word stands against בַּעַל אִשָּׁה; in v. 4 against הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ, who stay while "he goes out בְגַפּוֹ." Every ancient translation gives "alone": Onkelos בִּלְחוֹדוֹהִי; LXX μόνος; Rasag עאזבא, "unmarried."
- **Etymology: three proposals, all with the same result.**
  - *Body* (= בְּגוּפוֹ): the baraita at Kiddushin 20a (בְּגוּפוֹ נִכְנַס בְּגוּפוֹ יֵצֵא); Menachem (יתכן מלה זאת מגזרת 'גוף'); Ibn Ezra HaKatzar (כמו גופו); Radak, *Sefer HaShorashim*; Bekhor Shor; Reggio; Hirsch. Hoffmann reports that most moderns take this view.
  - *Skirt of the garment* (= בִּכְנָפוֹ): Rashi (שֶׁלֹּא בָּא אֶלָּא כְּמוֹת שֶׁהוּא, יְחִידִי, בְּתוֹךְ לְבוּשׁוֹ); Radak as an alternative; Ralbag; Shadal (a root meaning "cover," whence כָּנָף); Cassuto. Compare Gen 32:11, כִּי בְמַקְלִי עָבַרְתִּי. The Vulgate reads the garment in all three places.
  - *Back* (like גַּב): Ibn Ezra's long commentary, which gives "body" only as י"א: כי אין ראוי להיות פירושו רק שאין גוף אחר תלוי על גבו (cf. Ps 129:3). The Gra reaches "back" by the interchange of פ and ב.
- **"With his limbs intact"** (R. Akiva, below) is not the sense: the third occurrence sets בְגַפּוֹ against the woman and children who stay behind.

**בַּעַל אִשָּׁה הוּא.** בַּעַל + אִשָּׁה occurs twice, both in this code (21:3; 21:22 בַּעַל הָאִשָּׁה). The word order is the normal one and הוּא is required: in a nominal condition with a pronoun subject, predicate first and pronoun last is the order in 22 of 23 clauses (אִם־בֵּן הוּא, Ex 1:16; וְאִם שׁוֹר נַגָּח הוּא, Ex 21:29; וְאִם־נְקֵבָה הִוא, Lev 27:4; וְאִם־אִישׁ עָנִי הוּא, Deut 24:12). The exception is contrastive (Isa 66:9). Derashot from הוּא (Midrash, below) therefore rest on a word the sentence cannot do without.

**אִשָּׁה… אִשְׁתּוֹ.** One woman. An indefinite noun in the condition taken up by a suffixed noun in the outcome is how the laws refer back: כִּי־יִקַּח אִישׁ אִשָּׁה חֲדָשָׁה… וְשִׂמַּח אֶת־אִשְׁתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר־לָקָח (Deut 24:5); Deut 22:13, 19; Ex 21:26–27. (Pattern search; cases read.)

**אִשְׁתּוֹ: whom the Torah calls "his wife."**
- **A betrothed woman is "wife":** אֲשֶׁר־אֵרַשׂ אִשָּׁה וְלֹא לְקָחָהּ (Deut 20:7); the betrothed girl of Deut 22:23 is אֵשֶׁת רֵעֵהוּ in 22:24; אִשָּׁה תְאָרֵשׂ (Deut 28:30); אֶת־אִשְׁתִּי אֶת־מִיכַל אֲשֶׁר אֵרַשְׂתִּי לִי (2 Sam 3:14); Jacob's הָבָה אֶת־אִשְׁתִּי before the wedding (Gen 29:21).
- **A levirate widow is not:** she is אֵשֶׁת־הַמֵּת and יְבִמְתּוֹ until וּלְקָחָהּ לוֹ לְאִשָּׁה (Deut 25:5, 7).

**עִמּוֹ.**
- **Frequency.** עִם "with" occurs 1,049 times, אֵת "with" 878 times (Hebrew only). The share of אֵת: Torah 55% (243 / 294), Former Prophets 42%, Latter Prophets 80%, Chronicles and Ezra–Nehemiah 13%, other Writings 29%. Parallel passages switch in both directions: וַיַּשְׁלִמוּ אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל (2 Sam 10:19) // וַיַּשְׁלִימוּ עִם־דָּוִיד (1 Chr 19:19); עִם־פְּלִשְׁתִּים (2 Sam 21:19) // אֶת־פְּלִשְׁתִּים (1 Chr 20:5).
- **No difference of status.** Malbim (on 21:3, from *Kedoshim* §32) and HaKtav VeHaKabalah hold that עִם marks equality and אֵת subordination. The usage does not bear it out:
  - A wife going out with her husband takes אֵת: וַיֵּצֵא־נֹחַ וּבָנָיו וְאִשְׁתּוֹ וּנְשֵׁי־בָנָיו אִתּוֹ (Gen 8:18; also 6:18; 7:7; 8:16).
  - Dependants and servants take עִם: הוּא וּבָנָיו עִמּוֹ (Lev 25:41, 54); וּשְׁנֵי נְעָרָיו עִמּוֹ (Num 22:22); לְנַעֲרוֹ אֲשֶׁר־עִמּוֹ (1 Sam 9:5); וּשְׁתֵּי כַלֹּתֶיהָ עִמָּהּ (Ruth 1:7).
  - The same men take both in one story: הָאֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ (Gen 24:32) and הָאֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר־עִמּוֹ (24:54); Jacob's people, Gen 32:8 and 35:6. "The people who were with him": עִם 9, אֵת 25; "the men who were with him": 3 and 9 (pattern search on BHSA).
  - God is "with" a person with עִם 52 times and with אֵת 11 times, including וַיְהִי יְהוָה אֶת־יוֹסֵף (Gen 39:2, 21) and כִּי יְהוָה אִתּוֹ (39:3, 23).
  - Both can mean "equally with": וְעִם־אָדָם לֹא יְנֻגָּעוּ (Ps 73:5); אִתָּם יִמָּקּוּ (Lev 26:39).

**וְיָצְאָה.** HaKtav VeHaKabalah reads it as her *expenditure*, "her maintenance shall equal his," citing 2 Kgs 12:13 (וּלְכֹל אֲשֶׁר־יֵצֵא עַל־הַבַּיִת). There the subject is money; with a person as subject יָצָא means going out.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA):
1. [Conj אִם] [Adju בְּגַפּוֹ] [Pred יָבֹא]
2. [Adju בְּגַפּוֹ] [Pred יֵצֵא]: the outcome, without *vav*
3. nominal: [Conj אִם] [PreC בַּעַל אִשָּׁה] [Subj הוּא]
4. [Conj וְ] [Pred יָצְאָה] [Subj אִשְׁתּוֹ] [Adju עִמּוֹ]

**v. 3a is a mirror formula: he leaves as he came.** Compare עָרֹם יָצָאתִי מִבֶּטֶן אִמִּי וְעָרֹם אָשׁוּב שָׁמָה (Job 1:21); כָּל־עֻמַּת שֶׁבָּא כֵּן יֵלֵךְ (Eccl 5:15); Gen 9:6. In such formulas the repeated word keeps its sense (עָרֹם… עָרֹם), and a formula may repeat the word or use כֵּן. So the second בְּגַפּוֹ does not mean something different from the first (against Mizrachi and Siftei Chakhamim; Or HaChaim rejects the inference). Bekhor Shor and Chizkuni paraphrase with Ecclesiastes: כל עומת שבא כך ילך.

**v. 3b states a status, not when it began.** Standing after "if he comes in alone," the natural time is his entry: a man married when he was sold. The LXX makes this explicit: "if a wife comes in with him."

**v. 4's verb is in a condition, not a command** ([Conj אִם] [Subj אֲדֹנָיו] [Pred יִתֶּן] [Cmpl לוֹ] [Objc אִשָּׁה]). This bears on whether giving a bondwoman is permitted or required (Midrash, below).

**The *vav* on אִם does not mark levels.** vv. 3a, 3b and 4 open with bare אִם and v. 5 with וְאִם, but the second paragraph alternates (vv. 8 אִם, 9 וְאִם, 10 אִם, 11 וְאִם).

### Septuagint
Ἐὰν αὐτὸς μόνος εἰσέλθῃ, καὶ μόνος ἐξελεύσεται· ἐὰν δὲ γυνὴ συνεισέλθῃ μετʼ αὐτοῦ, ἐξελεύσεται καὶ ἡ γυνὴ αὐτοῦ.

*Brenton (1851). Rahlfs (1935) ends ἐξελεύσεται καὶ ἡ γυνὴ μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ, "the wife shall go out with him."*

- **μόνος**: "alone," without etymology.
- **ἐὰν δὲ γυνὴ συνεισέλθῃ μετʼ αὐτοῦ**: "if a wife comes in with him." The nominal clause becomes an entry, which fixes the marriage at the time of sale and has the wife enter the household.

### Vulgate
Cum quali veste intraverit, cum tali exeat: si habens uxorem, et uxor egredietur simul.

*Clementine Vulgate.*

- **Cum quali veste… cum tali**: "with such clothing as he came in, with such let him go out." The garment reading of בְּגַפּוֹ, as later in Rashi. In v. 4 likewise *exibit cum vestitu suo*.
- **si habens uxorem**: "if having a wife."

## Structure
- **v. 3a is the principle; v. 3b and v. 4 apply it.** A wife who came in with him goes out with him; a woman given to him during service stays. v. 4 closes with the principle's own words, וְהוּא יֵצֵא בְגַפּוֹ. Rashbam: אם בגפו יבא בגפו יצא - כולל ואח"כ מפרש; so too Chizkuni and Bekhor Shor. Hoffmann objects that the laws are too terse to repeat themselves, and that the reading disturbs his count of five laws.
- **The status words shift across vv. 3–5.** v. 3: "*his* wife"; v. 4: "*the* woman and *her* children"; v. 5, the slave: "*my* wife and *my* sons." See [21:4](04_verse.md).
- **Background: family debt servitude.** Wives and children held or sold for a man's debt: Hammurabi §117; 2 Kgs 4:1; Neh 5:5; Isa 50:1.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**The wife goes out: the master maintains her.**
- **MdRY Nezikin 1:24** (R. Yitzchak): who brought her in, that Scripture takes her out? Hence the verse teaches that the master must feed her, and, from Lev 25:41, the children.
- **Limits** (MdRY Nezikin 1:25; MdRShbY 21:3). אִשְׁתּוֹ excludes a levirate widow; עִמּוֹ excludes a betrothed woman (MdRShbY excludes both from עִמּוֹ). A wife forbidden to him is excluded: אשה שהיא ראויה להתקיים עמו. From הוּא, MdRShbY excludes a wife married without the master's consent, and gives the master his labour but not his family's: הוא, מעשה ידיו של רבו ואין מעשה בניו ובנותיו של רבו. His finds and inheritance are his own: אין לך עליו אלא עבודה.
- **Two wives.** MdRShbY 21:3 reads the repeated noun as covering a wife from before the sale and one taken after it (quoted by Ramban).
- **Do not separate them.** MdRShbY: ויצאה אשתו עמו, אל תפרישנו מאשתו; and from Lev 25:41, אל תפרישנו מבניו.
- *Assessment.*
  - **The duty to maintain her** is a reasonable legal inference, but R. Yitzchak's question has an answer in the plain sense: she came in with him. "Go out with him" presupposes that she was with him (Gen 8:18; Lev 25:41, 54; Ruth 1:7), and the LXX has her come in. So Ramban: כִּי הָיְתָה אֵשֶׁת הָעֶבֶד עִם בַּעְלָהּ כְּשִׁפְחָה לַאֲדֹנָיו; Shadal: לפי הפשט גם אשתו באה עמו בבית האדון; the Netziv; Cassuto. The question presupposes the halacha that a wife is not sold for her husband's theft.
  - **The limits from אִשְׁתּוֹ and עִמּוֹ match usage** (Words). The Torah calls a betrothed woman "wife" but not a levirate widow, so only "with him" can exclude the betrothed, who has not come in with him. Malbim grants the first half (הארוסה היא תמיד בכלל אשתו); his ground for the second, עִמּוֹ as equality, fails.
  - **Two wives** is not in the repetition (Words). Gur Aryeh: "בעל אשה" משמע שכבר היה לו אשה קודם שנמכר לעבד. A wife taken during service is a legal extension.
  - **The rulings from הוּא** attach legal content to a required word; they are not read from a superfluous one.

**Shadal's summary of the difference:** מכירת האשה והבנים הותרה בתורה שבכתב ונאסרה בתורה שבעל פה. He places it with the *ben sorer u-moreh*: the written law leaves the father his ancient powers, and the tradition removes them.

**The Netziv's reading.** The wife was never acquired. Used to the master's house, she might prefer to stay rather than leave for poverty with her husband; the verse requires her to leave with him and forbids the master to keep her.

**"Limbs intact."** MdRY Nezikin 1:19 (R. Akiva): אִם בְּרָאשֵׁי אֵבָרִים נִכְנַס, בְּרָאשֵׁי אֵבָרִים יֵצֵא: he is not freed by loss of an extremity but compensated. R. Yishmael: the verse is not needed for this. *Assessment:* not the sense of בְּגַפּוֹ (Words).

**Permission, not obligation** (v. 4's "if his master gives him a wife"). R. Yishmael derives it from v. 3a (MdRY Nezikin 1:18, 2:1); MdRShbY from אִם: יכול אף גזירה ת"ל אם אינו אלא רשות. *Assessment:* sound, and R. Yishmael's argument is from context. A condition alone does not settle the matter, since his own rule כָּל "אִם" וְ"אִם" שֶׁבַּתּוֹרָה, רְשׁוּת has three exceptions (see [21:9](09_verse.md)).
- **Malbim** explains why each tanna needs his reading: R. Yishmael holds a free Israelite may have a bondwoman and so needs v. 3a to show the master's gift is optional; R. Akiva holds she is forbidden (from לֹא יִהְיֶה קָדֵשׁ, as Onkelos renders it), so a command where a prohibition was assumed is only a permission (*Ayelet HaShachar* §§227–228), and בְּגַפּוֹ is free for the limbs reading. This is not shown. All three examples §227 gives for the rule (Num 5:14; Lev 21:3; Lev 25:46) are disputes in which R. Akiva holds חובה, the opposite (Sotah 3a–b). Sanhedrin 54a–b does not mention the bondwoman; the premise is Malbim's inference. And v. 4 contains no command.

### Talmud
**Kiddushin 20a.**
- **The baraita**: בְּגוּפוֹ נִכְנַס בְּגוּפוֹ יֵצֵא. Rava reads it as R. Akiva's limbs rule. Abaye answers that the law is already taught from 21:7 (לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת הָעֲבָדִים); Rava's reply, that the verse prevents him being both compensated and freed, is not in the word. Torah Temimah criticises the Kesef Mishneh (on Rambam, Avadim 4:6) for not finding this explicit: ודבר פלא הוא שדרשה מפורשת היא בגמ'.
- **R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov**: a slave without wife and children may not be given a bondwoman. Rav Nachman b. Yitzchak explains: יחידי נכנס יחידי יצא. So too the baraita at Temurah 30a.
  - *Assessment:* a received rule, not a reading. The formula says he leaves as he came; it does not restrict the master. v. 4 uses the same בְגַפּוֹ of a man who was given a woman; on this rule that man already has an Israelite wife, who goes out with him by v. 3b, yet he goes out "alone." Hoffmann raises this (והוא יצא בגפו קשה, שהרי לפי האמור יש לו אשה). Torah Temimah: the verse would have said בגפו יהיה. Mizrachi: מדוקיא דקרא נפקא ולא מפשטיה. Rambam: וְדָבָר זֶה קַבָּלָה הוּא (Avadim 3:4). In MdRShbY the same tanna reads the words as אין לך עליו אלא עבודה.
  - *Rationale.* Torah Temimah: a man with wife and children longs to be free for their sake, while a bondwoman given to a man with neither might bind him to her so that he never wants to leave.

**Kiddushin 22a.** R. Shimon: אִם הוּא נִמְכָּר – אִשְׁתּוֹ מִי נִמְכְּרָה? The same argument as R. Yitzchak's in the Mekhilta.

**Y. Shabbat 6:9.** Bar Kappara hurt his finger entering a town, then heard a child reciting אִם בְּגַפּוֹ יָבֹא בְּגַפּוֹ יֵצֵא, and took it as a sign that nothing worse would befall him there.

## Halacha
- **Rambam, Avadim 3:1.** The master maintains the Hebrew slave's wife and children, but not a betrothed woman, a levirate widow, or a wife forbidden to him: שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר אִשְׁתּוֹ עִמּוֹ אִשָּׁה הָרְאוּיָה לַעֲמֹד עִמּוֹ. On the verse: וְכִי תַּעֲלֶה עַל דַּעְתְּךָ שֶׁכֵּיוָן שֶׁנִּקְנָה זֶה נִשְׁתַּעְבְּדָה אִשְׁתּוֹ. אֲבָל לֹא בָּא לְלַמֵּד אֶלָּא שֶׁהָאָדוֹן חַיָּב בִּמְזוֹנוֹתֶיהָ. This applies to a wife and children from before the sale or after it, provided he married with the master's consent (following MdRShbY).
- **3:2.** Though he maintains them, the master has no claim on their labour or finds. Ramban disagrees: if the master maintains them, their earnings are his. Hoffmann reconciles the two: the master cannot compel their work, and if they forgo his support their earnings are their own.
- **3:4.** A slave without an Israelite wife and children may not be given a Canaanite bondwoman: וְדָבָר זֶה קַבָּלָה הוּא.
- **3:5.** Even when given a bondwoman, אֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לְהַפְרִישׁוֹ מֵאִשְׁתּוֹ וּבָנָיו שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שמות כא ג) "אִשְׁתּוֹ עִמּוֹ".
- **Sources**: MdRY Nezikin 1:24–25; MdRShbY 21:3; Kiddushin 20a, 22a. **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Cassuto** reads עֶבֶד עִבְרִי in v. 2 as already including the female slave, whose law is the same, which is why Deut 15:12 can name both.

### Ancient Near Eastern Context
- **Hammurabi §117.** In L. W. King's 1910 translation, a debtor's "wife, son, and daughter" are sold or given for debt and "work for three years in the house of the man who bought them." The wording of King's version ("sell himself, his wife…") needs checking against Roth's.
- **Nuzi.** Cassuto reports that a slave's wife could herself be a Hebrew bondwoman, and reads v. 3b so (not read at first hand).
