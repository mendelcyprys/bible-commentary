# אִם־רָעָ֞ה בְּעֵינֵ֧י אֲדֹנֶ֛יהָ אֲשֶׁר־לא [ל֥וֹ] יְעָדָ֖הּ וְהֶפְדָּ֑הּ לְעַ֥ם נָכְרִ֛י לֹא־יִמְשֹׁ֥ל לְמָכְרָ֖הּ בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָֽהּ׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Ketiv לא, qere לוֹ.** Written "who did *not* designate her," read "who designated her *for himself*."
- **The pair in the Leningrad Codex** (BHSA). לא written and לוֹ/לוּ read in 20 places: Ex 21:8; Lev 11:21; 25:30; 1 Sam 2:3; 2 Sam 16:18; 18:12; 19:7; 2 Kgs 8:10; Isa 9:2; 49:5; 63:9; Ps 100:3; 139:16; Job 6:21; 13:15; 41:4; Prov 19:7; 26:2; Ezra 4:2; 1 Chr 11:20. The reverse (לו written, לֹא read) in 2: 1 Sam 2:16; 20:2.
- **לא is an established spelling of *lô*.** A negative is impossible at Lev 11:21; 25:30; 2 Sam 16:18; 18:12; 19:7; 1 Chr 11:20 (a reading of each context), and 1 Chr 11:20 writes with לא the same sentence that 2 Sam 23:18 writes with לו. Isa 9:2 and Ezra 4:2 also favour לוֹ. Ex 21:8 is among the eleven where both readings make sense.
- **Accents.** The atnach on וְהֶפְדָּ֑הּ divides the condition and the positive duty (let her be redeemed) from the prohibition and its reason. The tevir on נָכְרִ֛י sets off the fronted phrase; בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָֽהּ closes the verse as the reason for the whole prohibition. No parenthesis is marked.

### Textual Variants
Not checked at first hand; as reported by Shadal and Hoffmann.
- **For the ketiv (negative):** Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion, the Syriac and the Samaritan Targum; perhaps Onkelos (אפשר שתרגם די לא יקיימינה ליה), and a targum manuscript dated 1411 reads דלא קיימה ליה (Shadal). Hoffmann: thirteen Onkelos manuscripts in De Rossi and one of Luzzatto's read לא.
- **For the qere:** printed Onkelos (דִיקַיְמַהּ לֵהּ), the Vulgate, Rasag, and the LXX (Rahlfs ἣν αὑτῷ καθωμολογήσατο). Hoffmann holds that among Greek witnesses only the Vatican manuscript and the Complutensian read "to himself," as corrections toward the qere (the Göttingen apparatus has not been checked).
- **Geiger** (*Urschrift*, 188) argued that the LXX preserves an original ketiv לו, later altered; Hoffmann rejects this.

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**יְעָדָהּ.** Root יעד, 29 occurrences. Qal 5: Ex 21:8, 9; 2 Sam 20:5 (הַמּוֹעֵד אֲשֶׁר יְעָדוֹ); Jer 47:7 (שָׁם יְעָדָהּ); Mic 6:9. Every qal is one agent appointing or assigning. The mutual sense, "meet by agreement," belongs to the niphal (19): הֲיֵלְכוּ שְׁנַיִם יַחְדָּו בִּלְתִּי אִם־נוֹעָדוּ (Amos 3:3); Job 2:11; Neh 6:2. Hirsch (יעד "related to יחד"; "The word itself which the Torah uses implies the idea of a mutual decision") reads the niphal's sense into a qal; the Kesef Mishneh's יעדה = ידעה by transposition has no grammatical basis.

**וְהֶפְדָּהּ.** פדה occurs 58 times: qal 53, niphal 3, hiphil 1, hophal 1. The hiphil is only here; the hophal only at Lev 19:20 (וְהָפְדֵּה לֹא נִפְדָּתָה), also a slave-woman law. The hiphil is causative, "he shall *let* her be redeemed": the one who acts is not the one who pays. It fits the master as subject (the father redeeming would be qal וּפָדָה), against Malbim's reading of the clause as addressed to the father, and it suits MdRShbY's "the court redeems her against his will." The LXX, Vulgate and Rasag make the master act; Rashbam at v. 11: ולא יניח לפדותה.

**לְעַם נָכְרִי.**
- **עַם** singular, without suffix, with an adjective: 67 occurrences (58 indefinite). Every one means a people, nation or multitude, including עַם אַחֵר (Deut 28:32; Ps 105:13). None means one person or a family.
- **עַם as "kin"** is real, but only with a suffix: "gathered to his kin," 9 times (עַמָּיו, Gen 25:8, 17; 35:29; 49:33; Num 27:13; 31:2; Deut 32:50 ×2; עַמִּי, Gen 49:29); plural עַם with a personal suffix 28 times.
- **נָכְרִי** (45) is usually "a foreigner"; it can mean a stranger to one's own family: נָכְרִיּוֹת נֶחְשַׁבְנוּ לוֹ (Gen 31:15, Rachel and Leah of their father); Ps 69:9. The phrase עַם נָכְרִי occurs only here.
- *Assessment.* "A foreign nation" is best supported by usage. "Any Israelite, foreign from her standpoint" is a permitted figurative extension. "Another clan" is possible but unparalleled in this form. "Another man" is unsupported; Malbim's objection holds: אולם לשון עם לא יצדק על איש אחד.

**לֹא יִמְשֹׁל לְמָכְרָהּ.** מָשַׁל (80) with לְ + infinitive occurs only here; its normal complement is בְּ (43). The verb is the vocabulary of dominion, and every translation reads "authority": he has no power to sell her. Whether a sale would be void (Malbim) is a legal inference. Legal "cannot" elsewhere is לֹא יוּכַל + infinitive (8 times, all in Deuteronomy); the Covenant Code has no comparable formula.

**בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ.** "Since he has broken faith with her."
- **Pointing.** בִּגְדוֹ is the ordinary form of "his garment" (12 times, e.g. Gen 39:12), and a rare form of the infinitive: GKC §61b, "sometimes takes the form qiṭl before suffixes, e.g. בְּבִגְדוֹ Ex 21." The vowels fit either. In the same verse לְמָכְרָהּ has the regular form, the contrast GKC points out.
- **Syntax decides.** בָּגַד (49) takes בְּ, "break faith with," 11 times, and בָּגַד + בָּהּ is exactly Mal 2:14, of a husband and wife: אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה בָּגַדְתָּה בָּהּ וְהִיא חֲבֶרְתְּךָ וְאֵשֶׁת בְּרִיתֶךָ. "His garment" cannot take בָהּ without a verb such as "spread." BHSA and GKC parse a verb; the LXX (ἠθέτησεν), Vulgate (*spreverit*) and Rasag (אד̇ גדר בהא) translate one.
- **Hirsch's etymology**: בגד ב־ is "to prove oneself as being merely a cloak towards someone": whoever fails the trust his clothed appearance invites is "an empty garment" (cf. מעל / מְעִיל).

**אֲדֹנֶיהָ.** "Her master," of the Hebrew maidservant; the same form as v. 4 (see [21:4](04_verse.md)).

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA, with the qere):
1. אִם רָעָה בְּעֵינֵי אֲדֹנֶיהָ
2. relative: [אֲשֶׁר] [Cmpl לוֹ] [Pred+Obj יְעָדָהּ]
3. [וְ] [Pred+Obj הֶפְדָּהּ]
4. [Cmpl לְעַם נָכְרִי] [Neg] [Pred יִמְשֹׁל]
5. infinitive: לְמָכְרָהּ
6. infinitive: [Pred+Subj בְּבִגְדוֹ] [Cmpl בָהּ]

**Fronting before a negated verb is ordinary focus, not a parenthesis.** In the laws (Ex 20–23; Leviticus; Numbers; Deut 12–26), of 621 negated *yiqtol* clauses, 416 begin with לֹא and 205 place a constituent first (100 an object, 38 a complement, 34 a subject). Complements placed first: וּמִיַּד בֶּן־נֵכָר לֹא תַקְרִיבוּ (Lev 22:25); כְּמַעֲשֵׂה אֶרֶץ־מִצְרַיִם לֹא תַעֲשׂוּ (Lev 18:3); שְׁאֵרָהּ כְּסוּתָהּ וְעֹנָתָהּ לֹא יִגְרָע (Ex 21:10). Malbim's reading of לְעַם נָכְרִי as a parenthesis, yielding two prohibitions, fails as syntax.

**A series of fronted ל-phrases in vv. 8–9**, on the qere: אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ יְעָדָהּ (for himself); לְעַם נָכְרִי לֹא יִמְשֹׁל (to a foreign people); וְאִם־לִבְנוֹ יִיעָדֶנָּה (for his son). BHSA gives the first and third the same structure. This favours the qere without deciding it: Malbim reads v. 9's contrast against לְעַם נָכְרִי, which works with the ketiv too. With the translations, all reading the qere, the balance favours the qere.

**Subject continuity.** The master is the subject from רָעָה בְּעֵינֵי אֲדֹנֶיהָ on; no new subject is named before לֹא יִמְשֹׁל or בְּבִגְדוֹ. The father was last named in v. 7. The grammar therefore leans to the master as the one warned; Hirsch's argument for the father (the master has no right to sell her at all) is legal, not grammatical.

**The spelling arguments.** Hoffmann argues that the qere only says לא here means לו, as in "fifteen other places" (ט"ו מקומות), and that no one would have changed an original לו; so the negative is original. Shadal also holds the negative original. The spelling is original, but it is an established spelling of *lô* (Masoretic Notes), so it cannot show that a negative was meant. Hirsch reads the direction of the pair as meaningful: "as the קרי is לו and the כתיב is לא and not the other way round… it is evident from the wording too that מצות יעוד קודמת." But לא written / לו read is the normal direction (20 of 22). Cassuto: the aleph marks a final long vowel, as in the Dead Sea Scrolls; proven as a mechanism, it does not decide this verse.

*Clementine Vulgate.*

- **cui tradita fuerat**: "to whom she had been handed," without a negative.
- **populo alieno**: "a foreign people."
- **si spreverit eam**: "if he has spurned her," a verb.

**Aramaic and Arabic.** Onkelos (printed): דִיקַיְמַהּ לֵהּ, the qere; וְיִפְרְקִנַהּ; לִגְבַר אָחֳרָן, "another man"; לֵית לֵהּ רְשׁוּ; בְּמִשְׁלְטֵהּ בַהּ, a paraphrase. Rasag: אן יתזוג̇ בהא (qere sense); פליפדהא; לבעץ̇ קום גריב, "to some foreign people"; לא יתסלט; אד̇ גדר בהא, "since he betrayed her." Every translation checked reads the qere and "authority"; all but Onkelos read "people/nation" and a verb of betrayal.

## Structure
- **The first sub-case of vv. 7–11** (אִם), followed by וְאִם (v. 9), אִם (v. 10), וְאִם (v. 11).
- **The sale frame.** v. 7: כִּי־יִמְכֹּר אִישׁ אֶת־בִּתּוֹ; v. 8: לֹא יִמְשֹׁל לְמָכְרָהּ. The father's sale opens the case; a second sale is forbidden.
- **A duty, then a prohibition, then the reason.** The resolution here is redemption, and the text does not say who pays; in v. 11 it is release, וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**Ketiv and qere: two packages.** Hoffmann organises vv. 8–11 by the rule יש אם למקרא / יש אם למסורת.
- **R. Akiva** reads the qere: אֲשֶׁר לוֹ יְעָדָהּ, he designated her; בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ, "after he spread his garment over her" (cf. Ruth 3:9), so the father may not sell her again. Designation is one option among others; nothing is betrayed if he does not designate.
- **R. Eliezer / R. Yishmael** read the ketiv: he bought her to designate her and did not; the betrayal is the broken undertaking. Every purchase presupposes an intent to marry. The same split appears at MdRY Nezikin 3:14 between R. Yose HaGelili (he may sell her only on condition of designation) and R. Akiva (he may sell her; if the master wishes, he designates).
- **Why each tanna reads as he does** (Malbim, on Kiddushin 18b). R. Eliezer, a disciple of Beit Shammai, holds that a man may divorce only for ערות דבר (Gittin 90a); he cannot read "designated her, then she displeased him," since that divorce would be forbidden, so he reads "did not designate." R. Akiva, who permits divorce even for finding another more beautiful, can read the qere. Second, R. Eliezer holds (Kiddushin 19b) that רָעָה means she was sold to men forbidden to her by a negative commandment, with whom betrothal still takes effect; R. Akiva holds betrothal does not take effect there, so he must read רָעָה plainly.
- *Assessment.* The grammar favours the qere (mildly) and betrayal (strongly): neither tanna's combination. "Who designated her for himself… since he has broken faith with her" is what the LXX, Vulgate and Rasag translate and what Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Ramban take as the plain sense. The packages were held together by legal commitments formed on other verses, not by the Hebrew.
- **Others.** Rashbam and Bekhor Shor read the negative. Shadal: העיקר לא באל"ף… אלא שהמקרא קצר, and he holds that scribes dropped the negative to match the qere. Hoffmann: ודאי צריכה כל ביקורת כנה להודות בכך שהגרסה 'לא' היא זו המקורית.

**Designation.**
- **Precedence.** MdRY Nezikin 3:13; MdRShbY: the duty to designate precedes the duty to redeem (also Bekhorot 13a). Tractate Avadim 1:6 ranks the claims of father and son.
- **Consent.** MdRShbY 21:8: יעדה — מדעתה… והוא הדין לכל הנשים שאין מתקדשות אלא מדעתם וברצונם. והלא דברים קל וחומר: ומה אמה עבריה שהיא כבושה תחת ידו, אמרה תורה יעדה מדעתה — שאר נשים לא כל שכן! The consent of every woman to her betrothal is argued *a fortiori* from the least free. Torah Temimah: ואמנם כל עיקר דין זה הוא ע"ד הדרש שסמכו זה על פסוק זה, דפשטות הכתוב מורה על יעוד שהוא כעין אירוסין. *Assessment:* derash; the qal of יעד is one-sided (Words).
- **Formula.** MdRShbY: he says before two witnesses הרי את מקודשת לי, and gives no betrothal money.
- **Timing.** Malbim: since the master may designate until the end of the six years, the father's duty to redeem could never be enforced; it becomes enforceable when the master designates another woman, since he may not designate two at once.

**The court may redeem her.** MdRShbY: והפדה — מלמד שבית דין מפדין אותה בעל כרחו. MdRY: redemption by deduction from the price.

**"To a foreign people."**

| Reading | Held by |
|---|---|
| A foreign nation (a gentile) | Ramban; Malbim; the Mekhilta as emended by the Gra |
| Another man | Onkelos; Rambam (Avadim 4:10); Torah Temimah, from Yevamot 63b |
| Another clan or kin-circle | Shadal (1839); Cassuto; Hoffmann; Hirsch |
| Any Israelite, foreign from her standpoint | Abarbanel; Malbim (second reading) |

- **Shadal**: עַם can mean a tribe or clan (Gen 28:3; 48:4, 19; Deut 33:19), נָכְרִי a stranger within Israel (Prov 5:10; 27:2); once genealogies were forgotten every Israelite became a stranger to her, "and therefore Chazal had to explain לעם נכרי as 'another man'." Cassuto: לעם נכרי, כלומר למשפחה זרה (המלה עם באה כאן בהוראה ארכאית). Hoffmann cites וְנֶאֱסַפְתָּ אֶל־עַמֶּיךָ (Num 27:13).
- **Hirsch**: "Each family circle is עם and עם נכרי is a family circle which would be permanently 'strange' to her," from the plural עַמָּיו. The father "has not the right to sell her into a circle which must, or should, remain srange to her"; hence no sale to relatives she cannot marry (Kiddushin 18b).
- **Torah Temimah** answers Ramban's objection that "another man" has no parallel (ולא מצינו לו חבר בכל הכתוב) from Yevamot 63b, where לְעַם אַחֵר (Deut 28:32) is expounded as the father's wife. That is a derashah on a verse whose plain sense is "another people."
- **Malbim's humane reading** of the Israelite case: כי כבר הורגלה בבית אדוניה וכאלו הוא עמה ומולדתם, וכשיוציאנה לבית אחר נחשב אצלה כמוסר אותה לעם אחר, כי זה נעשה ע"י בגידה. Abarbanel says the same.
- *Assessment.* See Words: nation > any Israelite (figurative) > clan (unparalleled) > another man (unsupported).

**Who is warned.** The father: MdRY Nezikin 3:15 (as emended by the Gra), Ramban, Hirsch, Malbim. Ramban's case: a father who sells her to a gentile for a year or two to get her away from a master who will not designate her. The master: Rambam, Avadim 4:10; Torah Temimah, because בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ fits the master who failed to designate. Both: Rashi (Kiddushin 18a). *Assessment:* the grammar leans to the master (Grammar).

**Deut 21:14** (Hoffmann): the captive woman he no longer wants is released outright, ומכר לא תמכרנה בכסף, because he paid nothing for her; here he may require redemption by deduction, but may not sell her on.

### Talmud
- **Kiddushin 18b.** R. Akiva: כיון שפירש טליתו עליה; R. Eliezer: כיון שבגד בה. The Gemara: R. Eliezer holds יש אם למסורת, R. Akiva יש אם למקרא. Also: not sold to relatives she cannot marry.
- **Kiddushin 18a.** Rashi: the warning addresses both father and master. The father may not sell her into servitude after marriage (Torah Temimah on בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָהּ, relying on the qere).
- **Kiddushin 19a–b.** Was the purchase money betrothal money? R. Yose b. R. Yehudah: no; to designate he must give new money, and a *perutah*'s worth of service must remain. The Sages: yes; he may designate until sunset of the last day. "Who did not designate her" teaches that he must inform her (יעדה / ידעה). Can the father stipulate against designation? R. Meir: the condition stands; the Rabbis: void (Tractate Avadim 1:5 and the Yerushalmi report R. Meir the other way).
- **Y. Kiddushin 1:2.** R. Ze'ira: he designates her by speech alone. If the betrothal money was given at the start, her earnings should belong to her father; the Yerushalmi resolves that the sale assigns them to the master.
- **Tosafot (Kiddushin 5b), Rashba, Ritva**: the master need only inform her; designation is valid even against her will (Torah Temimah, against the Kesef Mishneh).
- **Yevamot 63b.** Rav Chanan bar Rava on בָּנֶיךָ וּבְנֹתֶיךָ נְתֻנִים לְעַם אַחֵר: the father's wife.

## Halacha
- **Rambam, Avadim 4:7.** Designated to the master or his son she is like any betrothed woman, leaving only by death or a *get*. וּמִצְוַת יִעוּד קוֹדֶמֶת לְמִצְוַת פְּדִיָּה. Formula: הֲרֵי אַתְּ מְקֻדֶּשֶׁת לִי before two witnesses, even at the end of the six years near sunset, with no new money. Not two at once: שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שמות כא ח) "יְעָדָהּ".
- **4:8.** אֵין הָאָדוֹן מְיַעֵד אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה לֹא לוֹ וְלֹא לִבְנוֹ אֶלָּא מִדַּעְתָּהּ. The Kesef Mishneh reads this as her willing consent; Torah Temimah cites Tosafot, Rashba and Ritva for notice only.
- **4:9.** Designation is like betrothal, not marriage: he does not become impure for her, inherit her, or annul her vows until the wedding.
- **4:10.** אֵין הָאָדוֹן יָכוֹל לִמְכֹּר אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה וְלֹא לִתְּנָהּ לְאִישׁ אַחֵר בֵּין רָחוֹק בֵּין קָרוֹב וְאִם מָכַר אוֹ נָתַן לֹא עָשָׂה כְּלוּם; likewise a Hebrew bondman. Rambam's own reason for the verse: וְיֵרָאֶה לִי שֶׁלֹּא הֻצְרַךְ הַכָּתוּב לֶאֱסֹר דָּבָר זֶה [בְּאָמָה] אֶלָּא מִפְּנֵי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ לְיַעֲדָהּ לִבְנוֹ.
- **4:16.** A father's stipulation against designation is void, as contrary to the Torah.
- **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **The ketiv as original.** Geiger (*Urschrift*, 188) argued that the LXX preserves an original לו; Hoffmann replied that the Greek and Aramaic witnesses show לא as original and לו as the reading tradition. Shadal likewise holds the ketiv original. The corpus evidence (Masoretic Notes) makes the spelling neutral.
- **Cassuto** reads the whole sub-case as concubinage: a girl who was never married cannot be divorced, so if she displeases him she may redeem herself and go free; he cannot sell her to a strange family, מכיון שמכירה כזו תהיה מעין בגידה במי שהיתה שוכבת חיקו.
