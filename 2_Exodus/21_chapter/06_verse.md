# וְהִגִּישׁ֤וֹ אֲדֹנָיו֙ אֶל־הָ֣אֱלֹהִ֔ים וְהִגִּישׁוֹ֙ אֶל־הַדֶּ֔לֶת א֖וֹ אֶל־הַמְּזוּזָ֑ה וְרָצַ֨ע אֲדֹנָ֤יו אֶת־אָזְנוֹ֙ בַּמַּרְצֵ֔עַ וַעֲבָד֖וֹ לְעֹלָֽם׃ (ס)

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Paragraphing.** A *setumah* follows this verse and closes the law of the Hebrew slave (vv. 2–6).
- **Accents.** The zaqef qatan on אֶל־הָ֣אֱלֹהִ֔ים ends the first bringing. The atnach on אֶל־הַמְּזוּזָ֑ה divides the two bringings from the piercing and the service. אוֹ carries a disjunctive (tipcha), so "or to the doorpost" is its own unit.

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים.** "To God."
- **Usage.** אֱלֹהִים occurs 2,601 times, 376 with the article (61 in the Torah). אֶל/עַד הָאֱלֹהִים occurs 21 times; apart from Ex 21:6 and 22:7–8, all 18 mean God or gods: speaking or praying to God (Gen 17:18; 20:17; Ex 3:11, 13), crying out (Ex 2:23), going up to God (Ex 19:3; 1 Sam 10:3 עֹלִים אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים בֵּית־אֵל), consulting God (1 Sam 14:36 נִקְרְבָה הֲלֹם אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים, the verb of Ex 22:7).
- **Ex 18, the chapter that sets up the judges,** uses the phrase of God: וְהֵבֵאתָ אַתָּה אֶת־הַדְּבָרִים אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים (18:19). Onkelos keeps the distinction: at 18:19 לִקֳדָם יְיָ; at 21:6, 22:7, 8 לִקֳדָם דַיָּנַיָא; at 22:27 דַּיָּנָא.
- **The Covenant Code never uses שׁפט.** Its judicial terms are אֱלֹהִים (21:6; 22:7, 8 ×2, 27) and פְּלִלִים (21:22). Elsewhere in the Torah שֹׁפֵט/שֹׁפְטִים is used 10 times of human judges. Sanhedrin 3b notices this: R. Yoshiyah argues that otherwise Scripture would have said אֶל הַשּׁוֹפֵט.
- **The plural verb at Ex 22:8** (אֲשֶׁר יַרְשִׁיעֻן אֱלֹהִים) does not show human judges. Rebbi (MdRY Nezikin 15:15): 'אשר ירשיע' אין כתוב כן, אלא "אשר ירשיען", הא בשנים הכתוב מדבר. But Israel's God takes a plural verb at Gen 20:13; 35:7; 2 Sam 7:23; and Ps 58:12, אַךְ יֵשׁ־אֱלֹהִים שֹׁפְטִים בָּאָרֶץ, a plural participle of judging. The Chronicler changes 2 Sam 7:23 to a singular (1 Chr 17:21). The LXX renders 22:8 in the singular.
- **BDB** gives "rulers, judges… as divine representatives at sacred places" as sense 1a, with Ex 21:6; 22:7, 8, 27, and notes that Dillmann reads "God."
- *Assessment.* The word means God. That the slave was brought before God's judges is a sound inference from the setting (a code with no word for judge; a trial in 22:7–8), not the meaning of the word. The reading best supported by usage is Abarbanel's and Cassuto's: the place where God's judgment is given (Midrash, below).

**הַדֶּלֶת / הַמְּזוּזָה.**
- **מְזוּזָה**, 19 occurrences. In the Torah always a house doorpost (Ex 12:7, 22, 23; Deut 6:9; 11:20); a city gate (Judg 16:3); the sanctuary (1 Sam 1:9 עַל־מְזוּזַת הֵיכַל יְהוָה; 1 Kgs 6:31, 33; Ezek 41:21).
- **Door before doorpost is the fixed order** in all 5 verses where both occur (Ex 21:6; Judg 16:3; 1 Kgs 6:31; Isa 57:8; Prov 8:34). Malbim's ordering rule (*Ayelet HaShachar* §§199–200), by which the doorpost comes last to define the door as standing, explains nothing that needs explaining.
- **Whose door is not stated.** After אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים it can be the door of that place: Ibn Ezra (judges sat in the city gate, וזה טעם אל הדלת או אל המזוזה); Chizkuni (של שער ב"ד). Or the master's house (Rashbam, Bekhor Shor, Malbim, Hoffmann). Deut 15:17, with no sanctuary stage, can only mean the household's.

**אוֹ.** Joining two prepositional phrases, אוֹ occurs 83 times in the Torah, and all 83 give alternatives (e.g. בְּאֶבֶן אוֹ בְאֶגְרֹף, Ex 21:18; אֶל־הַכֹּהֵן… אוֹ אֶל־הַשֹּׁפֵט, Deut 17:12). At the head of a clause it means "or if" (67 times), as in Ex 21:36 אוֹ נוֹדַע and Lev 4:23 אוֹ הוֹדַע. Hoffmann, Torah Temimah and Chizkuni (או במקום אשר) read Ex 21:6's אוֹ as "if/which": "to the door that is by the doorpost." Their parallels are the clause-level construction; here אוֹ joins phrases. The idea of a single doorway has support from the LXX and Vulgate, which drop the "or," and from the targum variants (below), not from the conjunction.

**אֲדֹנָיו, repeated.** A subject noun renamed after one intervening subjectless *weqatal* clause occurs 13 times in the laws of Exodus–Deuteronomy (pattern search on BHSA): e.g. Lev 4:5–6 הַכֹּהֵן… הַכֹּהֵן; Num 5:16; Deut 22:15 אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָ… אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָ; 22:25. It is procedural style; here it also keeps master and slave distinct before "his ear."

**אָזְנוֹ.** Every ritual marking of an ear in the Torah specifies the tip of the right ear (7 of 7: Ex 29:20; Lev 8:23, 24; 14:14, 17, 25, 28). Ex 21:6 and Deut 15:17 specify neither side nor spot.

**בַּמַּרְצֵעַ.** Only here and Deut 15:17. The article is the one GKC §126q–r describes for a thing "present to the mind under given circumstances," with Ex 21:20 בַּשֵּׁבֶט among its examples: "with an awl." It neither restricts to a particular instrument nor extends to any instrument.

**וַעֲבָדוֹ.** Qal עבד with an object suffix is the ordinary way to name whom one serves: Jacob and Laban (Gen 29:15, 18; 30:26; 31:41); a people and its king (1 Sam 11:1; 1 Kgs 12:4). The six-year slave has the same suffix in the parallel law: וַעֲבָדְךָ (Deut 15:12; 15:18; Jer 34:14). The suffix cannot mean "him and not his son" (MdRY Nezikin 2:25; Malbim, *Ayelet HaShachar* §151; Hirsch: "Here it does not say ועבד as in V.2 but ועבדו"), since the six-year slave, who does serve the son, has it too.

**לְעֹלָם.**
- **In Lev 25, עוֹלָם marks what the Jubilee does not end:** גְּאֻלַּת עוֹלָם (25:32), אֲחֻזַּת עוֹלָם (25:34), and לְעֹלָם בָּהֶם תַּעֲבֹדוּ (25:46), the foreign slaves who are not released.
- **עֶבֶד עוֹלָם** is a permanent slave: Deut 15:17; 1 Sam 27:12; Job 40:28.
- **1 Sam 1:22**, cited for "fifty years": Hannah's עַד־עוֹלָם is glossed by Hannah herself at 1:28 as כָּל־הַיָּמִים אֲשֶׁר הָיָה, all the days he lives.
- *Assessment.* "For good," for life. Of the tannaitic readings, the master's lifetime fits the words best (Midrash, below).

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA): a chain of four *weqatal* clauses.
1. [Pred+Obj הִגִּישׁוֹ] [Subj אֲדֹנָיו] [Cmpl אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים]
2. [Pred+Obj הִגִּישׁוֹ] [Cmpl אֶל־הַדֶּלֶת אוֹ אֶל־הַמְּזוּזָה]
3. [Pred רָצַע] [Subj אֲדֹנָיו] [Objc אֶת־אָזְנוֹ] [Cmpl בַּמַּרְצֵעַ]
4. [Pred+Obj וַעֲבָדוֹ] [Time לְעֹלָם]

**Two bringings, then the piercing.** The Torah has 35 pairs of consecutive *weqatal* clauses with the same verb; apart from וְהָיָה, none restates one act (e.g. Num 4:10; 5:15; Ex 30:18; 40:6–7). So there are two stages, as Malbim, Hoffmann and Hirsch ("must have a purpose of its own") hold, and the LXX adds τότε, "then." The text does not say where the second stage is. Nor does it support the Yerushalmi's division of the two clauses between two slaves (court-sold and self-sold): one subject runs through the chain, and no new condition intervenes.

**The piercing surface.** Deut 15:17 וְנָתַתָּה בְאָזְנוֹ וּבַדֶּלֶת puts the awl through the ear into the door: the door is the backing. Ex 21:6 names door or doorpost as the place he is brought to. The Mekhilta's harmonisation (brought to either, pierced against the door) fits both.

## Translations
### Targum Onkelos
וִיקָרְבִנֵהּ רִבּוֹנֵהּ לִקֳדָם דַיָנַיָא וִיקָרְבִנֵהּ לְוָת דָשָׁא אוֹ לְוָת (נ״‎א דִילְוָת) מְזְזֶתָּא וְיַרְצַע רִבּוֹנֵהּ יָת אוּדְנֵהּ בְּמַרְצְעָא וִיהֵי לֵהּ עֲבַד פָּלַח לְעָלָם:

### Tafsir Rasag
**Judeo-Arabic:**

**Arabic script:**

### Septuagint
προσάξει αὐτὸν ὁ κύριος αὐτοῦ πρὸς τὸ κριτήριον τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ τότε προσάξει αὐτὸν ἐπὶ τὴν θύραν ἐπὶ τὸν σταθμὸν, καὶ τρυπήσει ὁ κύριος αὐτοῦ τὸ οὖς τῷ ὀπητίῳ, καὶ δουλεύσει αὐτῷ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα.

*Brenton (1851). Rahlfs (1935): τρυπήσει αὐτοῦ ὁ κύριος τὸ οὖς.*

- **πρὸς τὸ κριτήριον τοῦ Θεοῦ**: "to the tribunal of God." God is kept and the court added; closest to Abarbanel's and Cassuto's "place of judgment."
- **καὶ τότε**: "and then," making the two stages explicit.
- **ἐπὶ τὴν θύραν ἐπὶ τὸν σταθμὸν**: no "or"; one doorway.
- **εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα**: "forever."

### Vulgate
offeret eum dominus diis, et applicabitur ad ostium et postes, perforabitque aurem ejus subula: et erit ei servus in saeculum.

*Clementine Vulgate.*

- **diis**: "to the gods," the plural taken literally.
- **ad ostium et postes**: "to the door and the posts," no "or."

**Aramaic and Arabic.** Onkelos: לִקֳדָם דַיָנַיָא; לְוָת דָשָׁא אוֹ לְוָת מְזְזֶתָּא (a variant reads דִילְוָת, "that is by"); לְעָלָם. Rasag: אלי אלחאכם, "to the judge"; ללדהר, "for all time." Pseudo-Jonathan writes the halacha into the verse four times: לִקְדָם דַּיָּינַיָא וְיִסַּב מִנְהוֹן רְשׁוּתָא ("and take permission from them"); לְוַת דָּשָׁא דְּלוּחַ מְזוּזְתָא ("the door that is by the doorpost"); אוּדְנֵיהּ יַמִּינָא בְּמַחְטָא ("his right ear, with a needle"); עַד יוּבְלָא ("until the Jubilee"). It alone limits לְעֹלָם.

## Structure
- **vv. 5–6 are one sub-case**: the slave's declaration and a four-step consequence. The *setumah* closes the male-slave paragraph.
- **The parallel law:**

  | | Ex 21:6 | Deut 15:17 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Court / God | וְהִגִּישׁוֹ אֲדֹנָיו אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים | — |
  | Door | וְהִגִּישׁוֹ אֶל־הַדֶּלֶת אוֹ אֶל־הַמְּזוּזָה | — |
  | Act | וְרָצַע אֲדֹנָיו אֶת־אָזְנוֹ בַּמַּרְצֵעַ | וְלָקַחְתָּ אֶת־הַמַּרְצֵעַ וְנָתַתָּה בְאָזְנוֹ וּבַדֶּלֶת |
  | Result | וַעֲבָדוֹ לְעֹלָם | וְהָיָה לְךָ עֶבֶד עוֹלָם |
  | Woman | — | וְאַף לַאֲמָתְךָ תַּעֲשֶׂה־כֵּן (see [21:5](05_verse.md)) |

- **In both texts the master is the agent**: "his master" in Exodus, "you" in Deuteronomy.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**Elohim.**
- **The judges.** MdRY Nezikin 2:15, "that he might consult those who sold him." MdRShbY 21:6: מוליכו אצל שלשה ואומר דבריו בפניהם. So Onkelos, Rasag, Rashi, Rashbam, Ibn Ezra, Bekhor Shor, Shadal, Hirsch. Shadal gives the purpose: if the slave later regrets it, he cannot deny that he stayed willingly.
- **The place of judgment.** Abarbanel: וכבר ביארתי בסדר בראשית שלא קרא הכתוב לדיינים אלהים. אבל למקום המשפט לפי שהיו עושים שם משפטי האלהים יקראו למקום ההוא אלהים (cf. Deut 19:17). Cassuto: ההוראה הראשונה של המלה אלהים במסורת המשפטית הקדומה של המזרח היתה בוודאי פסילי האלילים הנמצאים בבית הדין, ובישראל נשאר הביטוי כנוסחה מאובנת להורות על מקום בית הדין. The Mekhilta itself considers an oracle at 22:7 (שומע אני לשאל באורים ותמים) and rejects it only from the plural verb.
- *Assessment.* The court as the procedure: well grounded. The court as the meaning of the word: not supported. Abarbanel's and Cassuto's "place" fits usage and the LXX best; Cassuto's images of the gods cannot be tested from the Hebrew corpus.

**Two stages, and where.** Malbim, attributing it to Rebbi: ממ"ש והגישו והגישו מבואר שלרציעה צריך הגשה אחרת, כי אינו נרצע כאן בב"ד, רק והגישו שנית בביתו אל הדלת וירצעהו בינו לבין עצמו. Hoffmann, crediting Hirsch, holds likewise that only the declaration is made in court, which explains Deuteronomy's omission of the stage. Against them, Sifrei Devarim 122:3 makes the court stage what keeps the act from being private. The Netziv: the judges are to rebuke him and dissuade him before the piercing proceeds. *Assessment:* two stages, yes (Grammar); "at home, privately," not in the text.

**Door and doorpost.** MdRY Nezikin 2:17; MdRShbY 21:6: as the doorpost stands in place, so must the door; the piercing is against the door (Deut 15:17). MdRShbY: a door without a mezuzah is included. Why the door must stand: Torah Temimah, because door and doorpost are witnesses (Kiddushin 22b), and witnesses stand (Deut 19:17); Y. Kiddushin 1:2, as a public disgrace to him and his family, who did not redeem him. Why wood: Rashbam (a public sign, and wood takes the awl); Bekhor Shor (houses are stone; ועוד כדי לפרסם הדבר); Cassuto: לא מפני איזו חשיבות מאגית של הדלת או של המזוזה כפי שרגילים לפרש, אלא פשוט כדי שיהיה מצע של עץ ראוי לסמוך עליו את האוזן.

**The master himself.** MdRShbY: אדוניו ולא בנו, אדוניו ולא עבדו, אדוניו ולא שלוחי אדוניו. MdRY Nezikin 2:18 makes it an exception to the rule that a man's agent is as himself. *Assessment:* the repetition of אֲדֹנָיו is ordinary style (Words), but both laws name the master as the one who pierces, so the conclusion stands as a legal rule attached to the wording.

**The ear.** Right ear, by *gezeirah shavah* with the leper (Lev 14:14, 25): MdRY Nezikin 2:19; MdRShbY; Sifrei Devarim 122:6. *Assessment:* an analogy that supplies what the text omits (Words, אָזְנוֹ). Which part of the ear:

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| MdRY Nezikin 2:20 | R. Yehudah: the lobe; R. Meir: also the cartilage, and a kohen is not pierced |
| MdRShbY 21:6 | the upper ear (בגבוה שבאוזן); R. Elazar reports R. Yudan for the lobe "lest he be a kohen," and adds: ואני אומר כל עצמו כהן אינו נרצע |
| Sifrei Devarim 122:5 | the Sages: the upper ear, since a lobe piercing could not blemish a kohen |
| Y. Kiddushin 1:2 | R. Yehudah b. R. Abun: the lobe; R. Meir: the cartilage |
| Tractate Avadim 3:1 | the attributions of R. Meir and R. Yehudah reversed |

Malbim: the dispute is about eligibility, not anatomy. At the lobe no blemish results, so a kohen is pierced; at the cartilage he is blemished, so he is not pierced, and (R. Meir in the Yerushalmi) not sold, on the principle stated in MdRY on 21:7 that one who cannot be pierced cannot be sold.

**With an awl.**
- **With anything.** MdRY Nezikin 2:22; MdRShbY (needle and other tools) and Kiddushin 21b attribute this to R. Yose b. R. Yehudah; Rebbi restricts it to metal.
- **"The tradition goes beyond Scripture."** Sifrei Devarim 122:1 (R. Yishmael): "in three issues the legal tradition goes beyond what is specified in Scripture," the awl being one (the translator notes that this passage may be a later scribal addition). In the received Sotah 16a the third case is the razor, not the awl; Rashi there cites a Yerushalmi version with the awl, and Sifrei attributes it to R. Shimon (Hoffmann). The Yerushalmi's framing (Kiddushin 1:2): במרצע אין לי אלא במרצע, מנין אפילו בסול אפילו בקוץ ובזכוכית? ת"ל ורצע. עד כדון כר"ע — כר' ישמעאל? תני ר' ישמעאל: בג' מקומות הלכה עוקבת מקרא.
- **Malbim**: R. Akiva can derive "anything" from ורצע by inclusion; R. Yishmael's method (כלל ופרט) can only include things like the specified item, so he must attribute the rule to tradition against the plain word; Rebbi does not accept the doctrine and restricts the awl to metal.
- **Hoffmann's inference**: the halacha had to state "with anything" against the wording, which shows that the Sages otherwise read these verses by their wording: עצם העובדה שצריך היה לקבוע את ההלכה "בכל דבר" ושלא כלשון הכתוב, מוכיחה את צדקת רבותינו כאשר הבינו את יתר דברי הפסוקים האלה כלשונם דווקא.

**Forever.**
- **Until the Jubilee.** MdRY Nezikin 2:23: if money, which buys anything, buys only six years, piercing cannot buy more; Lev 25:10 (וְשַׁבְתֶּם) bounds it. Rebbi (2:24): "forever" is fifty years (with 1 Sam 1:22). Abarbanel: fifty years is one generation. Pseudo-Jonathan: עַד יוּבְלָא. Hoffmann holds there is no conflict with the Jubilee law.
- **The master's lifetime.** Sifrei Devarim 122:8: "as long as the master is [alive] in his world (`olamo), even if he is sold thirty or forty years before the Jubilee." MdRShbY 21:6: ועבדו לעולם כל ימי עולמו של רוצע אפילו נמכר שלושים וארבעים שנה לפני היובל. (Midrash Tannaim on Deut 15:17 reads כל ימי עולמו של יובל.)
- **Malbim** reconciles them through Rebbi: עוֹלָם was chosen, rather than "until the Jubilee," because it covers a long fixed span, either the Jubilee period or the master's life; hence the Mishnah's two releases.
- **For life (peshat).** Rashbam: "According to the plain meaning of the text, 'for the rest of his life,'" citing 1 Sam 1:22. Shadal: ועבדו לעולם: כמשמעו, אלא שאח"כ נאמרה פרשת היובל, ופסלה במקצת מה שנאמר קודם לכן. Cassuto: כל ימי חייו, comparing Ugaritic *dʿlm*.
- **Hirsch**: לְעֹלָם means that "as far as he is concerned" he serves forever; he has lost the power to redeem himself, and release can come only from outside (the master's death, the Jubilee). Not in the word, which is a duration.
- *Assessment.* The word means for life (Words). Of the halachic readings, the master's lifetime fits it; the Jubilee limit comes from Lev 25:10, as the Mekhilta says.

**Not the son.** MdRY Nezikin 2:25; MdRShbY; Sifrei Devarim 122:8 by *gezeirah shavah* לעולם/עולם. Torah Temimah's reason: the piercing rests on the slave's love, "because it is good for him *with you*"; he never bound himself to the heirs. *Assessment:* the suffix does not carry it (Words), but the absence of any inheritance clause does: where slaves pass to heirs the Torah says so (Lev 25:46 וְהִתְנַחַלְתֶּם אֹתָם לִבְנֵיכֶם).

**By day.** Sifrei Devarim 120:2: the piercing is done by day, from הַיּוֹם (Deut 15:15).

**Shadal's comparative note.** Travellers report Persians and Indians piercing children's ears to dedicate them to idols, and such children are called *dāsa*, "slave"; perhaps the Torah made the piercing a mark of disgrace to distance Israel from the practice.

### Talmud
- **Sanhedrin 3b.** R. Yoshiyah: אֱלֹהִים in the Covenant Code refers to judges; otherwise Scripture would have said אֶל הַשּׁוֹפֵט. Rebbi's argument from the plural verb (Ex 22:8).
- **Kiddushin 21b.** "With anything" (R. Yose b. R. Yehudah) against metal only (Rebbi).
- **Kiddushin 22b: why the ear, why the door.**
  - R. Yochanan b. Zakkai: the ear that heard at Sinai כִּי לִי בְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל עֲבָדִים and went and acquired a master for itself (the self-seller); in MdRY Nezikin 2:21 and Tosefta Bava Kamma 7:2, the ear that heard "you shall not steal" and went and stole (the thief). Y. Kiddushin 1:2 has a third version: the ear that heard "you shall have no other gods." Torah Temimah prefers the Yerushalmi's: כִּי לִי בְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל עֲבָדִים (Lev 25:55) was never heard by the people at Sinai, while the first commandments were (Makkot 24a).
  - R. Shimon b'Rabbi: מה נשתנה דלת ומזוזה מכל כלים שבבית? אמר הקב"ה: דלת ומזוזה שהיו עדים במצרים… והלך זה וקנה אדון לעצמו, ירצע בפניהם.
  - Tosafot cite a midrash that מַרְצֵעַ has the numerical value 400, the years of servitude in Egypt. Torah Temimah suggests why: the Gemara's reason invites the question why every transgressor is not pierced, since all heard at Sinai; the allusion to Egypt answers it.
- **Kiddushin 15a.** Both וְשַׁבְתֶּם (Lev 25:10) and לְעֹלָם are needed: the first alone would free only one pierced shortly before the Jubilee; the second makes him serve until the Jubilee even beyond six years.
- **Y. Kiddushin 1:2.** The two bringings split between two slaves: the court-sold slave to the judges, the self-seller to the door. (The Bavli and Rambam require both stages for one slave.)
- **Sotah 16a.** R. Yishmael's three cases where halacha goes beyond Scripture (the text variants above). Torah Temimah on Tosafot at Sotah 23b: they should have cited MdRY on v. 7, that a daughter is not sold for theft because she is not pierced.

## Halacha
- **Mishnah Kiddushin 1:2**: הַנִּרְצָע נִקְנֶה בִרְצִיעָה, וְקוֹנֶה אֶת עַצְמוֹ בַיּוֹבֵל וּבְמִיתַת הָאָדוֹן.
- **Rambam, Avadim 3:6.** Only the court-sold slave is pierced; he serves to the Jubilee or the master's death.
- **3:7.** He does not serve the son: מִפִּי הַשְּׁמוּעָה לָמְדוּ (שמות כא ו) "וַעֲבָדוֹ", לוֹ וְלֹא לִבְנוֹ. (שמות כא ו) "לְעוֹלָם", לְעוֹלָמוֹ שֶׁל יוֹבֵל.
- **3:8.** A kohen is not pierced, since he would be blemished.
- **3:9.** The procedure: מְבִיאוֹ לְבֵית דִּין שֶׁל שְׁלֹשָׁה וְאוֹמֵר דְּבָרָיו לִפְנֵיהֶם, then to the door or doorpost while standing in a building; the right ear; בְּמַרְצֵעַ שֶׁל מַתֶּכֶת, through to the door; by the master himself, not his son, agent or the court's agent; not two slaves at once.
- **Sources**: MdRY Nezikin 2:15–25; MdRShbY 21:6; Sifrei Devarim 120, 122; Kiddushin 14b–15a, 21b–22b; Y. Kiddushin 1:2. **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Deuteronomy's omission of the sanctuary stage.** Farber (TheTorah.com): in Exodus the piercing is a rite at a local sanctuary (אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים); Deuteronomy, which centralises worship, makes it domestic. Hoffmann's alternative: the declaration was always in court and the piercing at home, so Deuteronomy records only the second stage.
- **Cassuto** reads אֱלֹהִים as a survival of an older legal formula in which the images of the gods stood in the court.

### Ancient Near Eastern Context
- **Ugaritic *dʿlm***, "of eternity," a perpetual slave (Cassuto, on לְעֹלָם).
- **Hammurabi**: Cassuto notes that a slave who rebelled against his master had his ear cut off.
