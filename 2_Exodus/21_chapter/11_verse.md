# וְאִם־שְׁלָ֨שׁ־אֵ֔לֶּה לֹ֥א יַעֲשֶׂ֖ה לָ֑הּ וְיָצְאָ֥ה חִנָּ֖ם אֵ֥ין כָּֽסֶף׃ (ס)

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Paragraphing.** A *setumah* follows this verse and closes the unit 21:1–11.
- **Accents.** The zaqef qatan on אֵ֔לֶּה sets off the fronted object "these three." The atnach on לָ֑הּ divides condition from consequence. The tipcha on חִנָּ֖ם divides "she shall go out free" from "no money": the accents make two clauses, not one phrase.

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**שְׁלָשׁ אֵלֶּה.** A number joined to אֵלֶּה occurs 6 times. Five point back: to a list just given (שְׁלֹשָׁה אֵלֶּה בְּנֵי־נֹחַ, Gen 9:19; שְׁמֹנָה אֵלֶּה, Gen 22:23); to a series of episodes (אֶת־אַרְבַּעַת אֵלֶּה יֻלְּדוּ לְהָרָפָה, 2 Sam 21:22, summing up four battle reports, each opened by וַתְּהִי־עוֹד מִלְחָמָה); to a group named earlier (Zech 4:10); and Ex 21:11. One points forward (Isa 47:9).
- **Three alternatives** can be called "three," but the choice is then marked: שָׁלֹשׁ אָנֹכִי נוֹטֵל עָלֶיךָ בְּחַר־לְךָ אַחַת־מֵהֶם (2 Sam 24:12). In law, "any one of these" is marked with אַחַת מֵאֵלֶּה (Lev 5:4, 5, 13) or בְּכָל־אֵלֶּה (Lev 18:24; Hag 2:13). This verse has neither.

**יַעֲשֶׂה לָּהּ.** In a Torah law about a woman, only here, at v. 9 (כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת יַעֲשֶׂה־לָּהּ) and in the sotah passage (Num 5:30). v. 11 picks up v. 9's wording.

**חִנָּם.** 32 occurrences: without payment 10, without cause 18, in vain 4. All four in the Torah mean "without payment" (Gen 29:15; Ex 21:2, 11; Num 11:5); "without cause" begins in Samuel.

**אֵין כָּסֶף.** The two words stand together only here and at 1 Kgs 10:21 // 2 Chr 9:20, in another sense. Elsewhere "no money" names who lacks it: אֵין־לוֹ כָּסֶף (Isa 55:1); 2 Sam 21:4. חִנָּם and כֶּסֶף are never adjacent elsewhere: at Isa 52:3, חִנָּם נִמְכַּרְתֶּם וְלֹא בְכֶסֶף תִּגָּאֵלוּ, they are in parallel clauses about two different acts. The code uses a short אֵין-clause for a legal consequence: וְהֻכָּה וָמֵת אֵין לוֹ דָּמִים (Ex 22:1). The phrase is not a hendiadys.

**וְיָצְאָה.** With a woman as subject in Torah law: Ex 21:3 (a wife leaving with her husband); 21:7; 21:11; Deut 24:2, וְיָצְאָה מִבֵּיתוֹ, of a divorced wife. So the verb suits a wife as well as a maidservant.

**No חָפְשִׁי.** The word is used of released slaves at Ex 21:2, 5, 26–27 (including a Canaanite אָמָה), at Deut 15:12–18 (including the Hebrew woman) and at Jer 34:9–16. The daughter of vv. 7–11 is the only released woman in these laws never called חָפְשִׁי: she "goes out" but is not "set free." This fits a marriage-shaped arrangement rather than manumission; it proves nothing alone.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA):
1. [וְאִם] [Objc שְׁלָשׁ אֵלֶּה] [Neg] [Pred יַעֲשֶׂה] [Cmpl לָהּ]
2. [וְ] [Pred יָצְאָה] [Modi חִנָּם]
3. nominal: [אֵין] [כָּסֶף]

**vv. 10 and 11 have the same structure**: fronted object + לֹא + *yiqtol*. שְׁלָשׁ אֵלֶּה stands in the slot that v. 10's three nouns occupy one verse earlier.

**One release, not two.** One verb and one adverb (וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם), followed by a consequence clause (אֵין כָּסֶף) of the Ex 22:1 type. When the code distinguishes two cases it states the condition that separates them: אֵין לוֹ דָּמִים… אִם־זָרְחָה הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ עָלָיו דָּמִים לוֹ (Ex 22:1–2). No condition separates חִנָּם from אֵין כָּסֶף.

**Why "no money" here and not in v. 2.** v. 2 says only חִנָּם. Between them stands v. 8's וְהֶפְדָּהּ, a release paid for; v. 11's release is the unpaid alternative, and "no money" says so. Abarbanel makes this link on reading B (below).

**Translations.** Onkelos: וְאִם תְּלָת אִלֵין לָא יַעְבֵּד לַהּ וְתִפּוֹק מַגָּן בְּלָא כְסַף, literal. Pseudo-Jonathan writes reading A into the text, לִמְזַמְנָא יָתָהּ לֵיהּ אוֹ לִבְרֵיהּ אוֹ לְמִפְרְקָא, and adds בְּרַם גֵּט פְּטוּרִין יָהִיב לָהּ. Rasag: פאן לם יצנע בהא ואחדה מן הד̇ה אלת̇לת̇, "one of these three." The translations that interpret supply a marker of alternatives, as Rashi (אִם אַחַת מִשְּׁלֹשׁ אֵלֶּה) and Ibn Ezra (הנכון שהוא ואם אחת משלש אלה) do. None splits חִנָּם and אֵין כָּסֶף into two events.

## Structure
- **The paragraph is one case and four sub-cases**, laid out like vv. 2–6:

  | Verse | Opens | Case |
  |---|---|---|
  | 7 | וְכִי | a man sells his daughter; she does not go out as the slaves do |
  | 8 | אִם | she displeases him: he must let her be redeemed |
  | 9 | וְאִם | he designates her for his son: he treats her as a daughter |
  | 10 | אִם | he takes another: her provision is not reduced |
  | 11 | וְאִם | "these three" he does not do: she goes out |

- **Exactly three sub-cases precede "these three,"** each ending in something the master must do for her; 2 Sam 21:22's "these four" sums up four preceding episodes the same way.
- **Frame.** The unit opens with release in v. 2 (יֵצֵא לַחָפְשִׁי חִנָּם) and closes with it in v. 11 (וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם). v. 7's לֹא תֵצֵא כְּצֵאת הָעֲבָדִים is answered by v. 11's וְיָצְאָה: she does not go out as the slaves do, but in this case she does go out. Cassuto: in both paragraphs one main case and four sub-cases, בסך הכל בשתי הפיסקות עשרה סעיפים על דיני עבדות, מספר העוזר לזכרון, כמספר עשרת הדיברות.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**What are "these three"?**
- **(A) Designate her for himself, designate her for his son, or let her be redeemed.** MdRY Nezikin 3:28; R. Akiva (MdRShbY); Rashi; Ibn Ezra (with Hag 2:13 בְּכָל־אֵלֶּה); Ramban; Rashbam (ולא יניח לפדותה); Bekhor Shor; Hirsch; Shadal; Rambam, Avadim 4:9. The Mekhilta: it cannot be food, clothing and onah, since the verse speaks of a maidservant not yet betrothed.
- **(B) Food, clothing and onah** (v. 10). R. Eliezer, MdRShbY 21:11: ר' אליעזר אומר: זה שאר כסות ועונה. אמר לו ר' עקיבה: מה אני צריך? והלא כבר נאמר שארה כסותה ועונתה לא יגרע! Held also by Chizkuni; the Netziv (ואם שלש אלה. שארה כסותה ועונתה); Cassuto (שאר כסות ועונה כרגיל). Hoffmann knew R. Eliezer's baraita only from Ibn Janah's *Sefer HaRikmah* (p. 214) and wrote that it לא נשתמרה בשום מקום אחר; the reconstructed MdRShbY now preserves it.
  - **Abarbanel** calls (A) הפירוש המקובל בפסוקים האלה והוא האמתי, then adds: האמנם כפי סגנון הפסוקים אפשר לפרש ואם שלש אלה לא יעשה לה — שהם הג' שזכר בסמוך, שארה כסותה ועונתה לא יגרע, on which she leaves at any time without redemption, "and that is אין כסף."
  - **Malbim**, defender of the derivations, grants (B) on R. Yoshiyah's premise: הלא יותר טוב לפרש ששלש אלה היינו שאר כסות ועונה, כי על יעוד ופדיון היל"ל ואם אחת משלש אלה.
  - **Against (B).** Shadal: אם כבר לקחה לו הרי היא אשת איש, ואיך תצא ממנו בלא ספר כריתות שתהיה מותרת לכל אדם? ומליצת ויצאה חנם אין כסף תצדק באמה ולא באשת איש. Hirsch: "Once she is his wife, she can only leave him by גט and, moreover, the חנם אין כסף could then have no meaning." The Mekhilta (Nezikin 3:29) answers in advance: חנם is free of money, not free of a *get*; so the Netziv: ואם היה כתוב חנם לחוד הייתי אומר דגם ג״פ א״צ… מש״ה פירש הכתוב… חנם אין כסף. אבל ג״פ היא צריכה זהו פשט המקרא. Torah Temimah takes the *get* here as a writ of manumission.
- **(C) The three preceding sub-cases**: redemption (v. 8), designation to the son (v. 9), keeping her with full provision (v. 10); none of these happened. Hoffmann, on the qere.
- *Assessment.* On the Hebrew: (C) fits the paragraph's design best; (B) fits where the words point (the nearest list of three, and the identical structure of vv. 10–11); (A) is coherent, since mutually exclusive options can be "three," but takes its items from vv. 8–9 while skipping v. 10, and every reader of it supplies "one of." Shadal's and Hirsch's objection to (B) is legal, not linguistic (Words, וְיָצְאָה).

**"Free" and "no money" as two releases.** MdRY Nezikin 3:30–32; MdRShbY; Y. Kiddushin 1:2; Kiddushin 4a.
- First tanna: וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם is maturity (*bagrut*); אֵין כָּסֶף is the signs of puberty (*na'arut*). R. Yonathan reverses them. R. Eliezer (through Abba Chanan): the surplus also teaches release by deduction. Rashi's text on the verse (ויצאה חנם with signs; אין כסף for *bagrut*) is inverted from Kiddushin 4a; Mizrachi was troubled; Torah Temimah explains Rashi's order by Bava Kamma 87a.
- **Malbim** ties the positions to Rabbah and Abaye (Kiddushin 4a) on whether one case can be learned from another within a single body: R. Yonathan, following Abaye, needs the second expression for an *ailonit*, who has no *na'arut*.
- **Hirsch** reconstructs the sense: חִנָּם alone would state the obvious, that service ends when the father's authority ends (*bagrut*); אֵין כָּסֶף, "there is to be no compensation," is needed only if she leaves when the master might have thought himself owed compensation, i.e. at *na'arut*, six months earlier; חִנָּם still covers the *ailonit*.
- **Ibn Ezra** declines the doubling: אֵין כָּסֶף is תוספת ביאור, like כִּי מֵת אַתָּה וְלֹא תִחְיֶה (2 Kgs 20:1). Chizkuni: לפי פשוטו יתור לשון. Torah Temimah on the ages: Chazal did not intend to load these periods into the words; they attached them (סמכו) to expressions superfluous to the matter.
- **Hoffmann's plain-sense route to the same result**: the father can transfer a right only while his own right lasts; he sells her as a minor hoping the master will marry her when she comes of age; as *na'arut* approaches the master must decide. Compare Pesachim 113a: איזהו עני רשע ערום? זה המשהה בתו בוגרת.
- *Assessment.* The most strained derivation in the unit: one release plus a consequence clause, with no condition dividing them (Grammar). Yet it yields the passage's strongest protection, release at puberty (Mishnah Kiddushin 1:2), and Malbim makes it the premise of further restrictions at v. 7: because she leaves at the signs she is not pierced, and because she is not pierced she is not sold for theft.

**Two more derivations from אֵין כָּסֶף.**
- **Ketubot 46b** (Rav Yehudah): אין כסף לאדון זה אבל יש כסף לאדון אחר, ומנו אביה: the father receives her betrothal money. *Assessment:* "no money to this master" is the plain sense, since the master is the understood party; "but to another there is" is derash. Rashi omits it.
- **Y. Kiddushin 1:2**: since she leaves without money, she was acquired with money. The Bavli derives it from וְהֶפְדָּהּ; Torah Temimah objects that the deduction proves nothing about the mode of acquisition, and ends ועדיין צ"ע ובאור.

**Further releases.** Bar Pedaya (Y. Kiddushin 1:2; cf. Kiddushin 17b): at the master's death, from Deut 15:17b. Reish Lakish (Kiddushin 4b): at her father's death, *a fortiori* from puberty; R. Hoshaya objects from the Mishnah's silence.

### Talmud
- **Kiddushin 4a.** ויצאה חנם אלו ימי בגרות, אין כסף אלו ימי נערות. Why both? Rabbah and Abaye on learning one case from another; the *ailonit*.
- **Kiddushin 4b.** Reish Lakish: she goes free at her father's death.
- **Ketubot 46b.** אין כסף לאדון זה אבל יש כסף לאדון אחר: the father's right to her betrothal money (Bava Kamma 87a derives it from בִּנְעֻרֶיהָ בֵּית אָבִיהָ instead).
- **Kiddushin 18a.** The designated maidservant leaves only by a *get* (Sifrei Devarim 118:5).
- **Pesachim 113a.** One who keeps his daughter unmarried until *bagrut* is called wicked (Hoffmann's social frame).

## Halacha
- **Mishnah Kiddushin 1:2**: יְתֵרָה עָלָיו אָמָה הָעִבְרִיָּה, שֶׁקּוֹנָה אֶת עַצְמָהּ בְּסִימָנִין.
- **Rambam, Avadim 4:5.** She goes free when she shows signs of puberty, even the day after she was bought: שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שמות כא יא) "וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם" רִבָּה לָהּ הַכָּתוּב יְצִיאָה אַחֶרֶת בְּחִנָּם יֶתֶר עַל הָעֶבֶד. מִפִּי הַשְּׁמוּעָה לָמְדוּ שֶׁהִיא הֲבָאַת סִימָנֵי נַעֲרוּת. An *ailonit* goes free at *bagrut*.
- **4:6.** Six modes of release: years, Jubilee, deduction, writ, the master's death, signs.
- **4:9.** (שמות כא יא) "וְאִם שְׁלָשׁ אֵלֶּה לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה לָהּ", לֹא יִעֲדָהּ לוֹ וְלֹא יִעֲדָהּ לִבְנוֹ וְלֹא נִפְדֵּית בְּגִרְעוֹן כֶּסֶף, וְיָצְאָה חִנָּם בַּהֲבָאַת סִימָנִין. Rambam follows reading (A).
- **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Geiger and Hoffmann.** Geiger (*Urschrift*, 187–188) read vv. 8–11 as preserving an old and a young halacha, with the ketiv/qere marking the seam. Hoffmann replies that the divergent interpretations follow the standing principles יש אם למקרא / יש אם למסורת, not historical strata. Geiger has not been read at first hand.
- **Koller** (TheTorah.com) charts the paragraph as one main case and four sub-cases, as Cassuto counts it.
