# אִם־אֲדֹנָיו֙ יִתֶּן־ל֣וֹ אִשָּׁ֔ה וְיָלְדָה־ל֥וֹ בָנִ֖ים א֣וֹ בָנ֑וֹת הָאִשָּׁ֣ה וִילָדֶ֗יהָ תִּהְיֶה֙ לַֽאדֹנֶ֔יהָ וְה֖וּא יֵצֵ֥א בְגַפּֽוֹ׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Accents.** The atnach on בָנ֑וֹת divides condition from consequence; the zaqef qatan on אִשָּׁ֔ה and on לַֽאדֹנֶ֔יהָ divides each half in two. The result is two by two: *he gives her* ǀ *she bears*; *she and her children stay* ǀ *he goes*.
- **הָאִשָּׁ֣ה וִילָדֶ֗יהָ.** הָאִשָּׁה has a conjunctive accent (munach) and the disjunctive (revia) falls after וִילָדֶיהָ. The Masoretes read "the woman and her children" as one subject phrase of תִּהְיֶה, not "the woman shall be her master's" with the children as an aside.

## Language

### Words
**אֲדֹנָיו… לַאדֹנֶיהָ.**
- **Form.** אָדוֹן takes the plural form before suffixes: אֲדֹנָיו 47 times, singular אֲדֹנוֹ once (Prov 30:10). Ibn Ezra (reported by Hoffmann): the first person is the exception, אֲדֹנִי (as in v. 5), to avoid confusion with the divine name.
- **"Her master"** with אָדוֹן occurs 4 times: Ex 21:4; 21:8; Judg 19:26, 27. Ex 21:8 is the Hebrew maidservant. A woman's mistress is גְּבִרְתָּהּ (Gen 16:4, 8, 9; Ps 123:2; Prov 30:23). The term marks a woman living under a man's authority in his household, not her ethnicity. Hirsch's argument, "as the master is called אדניה, 'her master,' it can only be speaking here of a non-Jewish slave," fails on Ex 21:8.
- **The switch** from "his master" to "her master" within the verse is natural once "the woman" is the subject; Ex 21:8 says "her master" where only one master exists. R. Nathan's reading, that her master may be someone else (Midrash, below), is permitted by the wording but not required.

**יִתֶּן־לוֹ אִשָּׁה.** The marriage idiom is נָתַן X לְ-Y לְאִשָּׁה (11 times), used even of maidservants: Hagar (Gen 16:3), Bilhah (30:4), Zilpah (30:9). The bare form without לְאִשָּׁה occurs twice: here and 1 Kgs 11:19, where Pharaoh gives Hadad a royal wife. The wording neither shows nor excludes a marriage.

**וְיָלְדָה־לוֹ בָנִים אוֹ בָנוֹת.** לוֹ grants his paternity; "sons or daughters" means any children. The Netziv explains why both are named: each could be thought the one who follows the father (ואת דינה בתו; הבת אחר האם, Ketubot 13:3).

**וִילָדֶיהָ.** יֶלֶד with a mother's suffix for a group of people ("X and her children") occurs only here and in Gen 33:2, 6, 7: the maidservants and their children, and Leah and her children, meeting Esau.

**The names for the woman and the children across vv. 3–5.**

| Where | Voice | Woman | Children |
|---|---|---|---|
| v. 3 (the free wife) | law | אִשְׁתּוֹ, *his* wife | — |
| v. 4, condition | law | אִשָּׁה, *a* woman | בָּנִים אוֹ בָנוֹת born *to him* |
| v. 4, ruling | law | הָאִשָּׁה, *the* woman | וִילָדֶיהָ, *her* children |
| v. 5 | the slave | אִשְׁתִּי, *my* wife | בָּנָי, *my* sons |

The ruling withholds the relational terms the slave himself uses a verse later: it grants paternity in the condition and assigns the children by their mother. Not remarked in the commentators checked (Hirsch, Cassuto, Malbim, Abarbanel, Hoffmann, Shadal, Torah Temimah, Netziv, Rashbam, Bekhor Shor, both Mekhiltas). Cassuto's paraphrase merges the voices: את אשתו ואת ילדיה.

**בְגַפּוֹ.** "Alone"; see [21:3](03_verse.md).

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses.** The condition is אִם + *yiqtol* (יִתֶּן) + *weqatal* (וְיָלְדָה). The consequence has two clauses, each with its subject before the verb: הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ תִּהְיֶה לַאדֹנֶיהָ / וְהוּא יֵצֵא בְגַפּוֹ. The pronoun הוּא is not needed for sense, so it marks contrast: *they* stay, *he* goes.

**The singular תִּהְיֶה marks the woman as the principal member.**
- **The problem.** A compound subject before its verb normally takes a plural, masculine if the genders are mixed (GKC §146d; Gen 18:11 אַבְרָהָם וְשָׂרָה זְקֵנִים). Here the subject is feminine singular + masculine plural and the verb is feminine singular.
- **Malbim** (*Ayelet HaShachar* §§166, 176–177) reads the singular as marking the first-named as essential and the rest as following her, and bases the children's status on it. His examples (ותשר דבורה וברק; Esth 9:29; Gen 8:18; Lev 9:23; Gen 25:9; 34:25) all have the verb *first*, where GKC §146f explains agreement with the first member by proximity (Gen 7:7; Num 12:1; Judg 5:1). They are the wrong construction.
- **The right comparison** is a verb *after* a subject of the shape "X and X's dependants." Fourteen cases found:

  | Agreement of the following verb | Cases |
  |---|---|
  | Singular, with the first member | Ex 21:4 הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ תִּהְיֶה; Lev 27:10 and 27:33 הוּא וּתְמוּרָתוֹ יִהְיֶה קֹּדֶשׁ; Ezek 16:46 שֹׁמְרוֹן הִיא וּבְנוֹתֶיהָ הַיּוֹשֶׁבֶת (participle) |
  | Plural, in the gender of the first member | Gen 33:6 הַשְּׁפָחוֹת הֵנָּה וְיַלְדֵיהֶן וַתִּשְׁתַּחֲוֶיןָ |
  | Plural | Gen 33:7; Ex 1:1; Lev 8:31; Num 4:19; Judg 18:30; 1 Sam 25:20; Ezek 27:9; Neh 4:16 |
  | With the second member | Jer 44:25 (GKC §146g emends with the LXX) |

- **What it shows.** The singular is the marked form (4 of 14). In Ex 21:4 it cannot be proximity: תִּהְיֶה agrees in gender with the farther member. Where the verb is singular, the predicate is a status the second member has because of the first (the substitute animal is holy because it replaced the consecrated one); where it is plural, each member acts in his own right. Lev 27:10, 33 is the same kind of law as Ex 21:4.
- **GKC §146e, independently**: "in Ex 21:4 (where הָֽאִשָּׁה וִֽילָדֶיהָ are the subjects) it agrees with הָֽאִשָּׁה as being the principal person."
- **An alternative.** Prov 27:9, שֶׁמֶן וּקְטֹרֶת יְשַׂמַּח־לֵב, has the same agreement for a pair treated as one thing. On that reading the phrase is "the woman-with-her-children, as one holding"; the woman still heads it.
- *Assessment.* Malbim's conclusion holds on better evidence than his, for the base case: within this verse the children's status runs through the mother. The extension to a gentile woman (Talmud, below) is the rabbis' step, not the grammar's. The search is small and not exhaustive.

**Malbim's general rule** (a singular verb with a compound subject marks one principal) is unproven as a rule. The variation is not random: the laying on of hands by Aaron and his sons keeps the same number from command to execution (Ex 29:10 singular, Lev 8:14 singular; Ex 29:15 plural, Lev 8:18 plural), and Malbim explains the one change (Ex 29:19 singular, Lev 8:22 plural) by the Tosefta's rule that the singular means one after another and the plural all at once. But a rule that can read a singular as either "principal" or "one at a time" can absorb almost any example.

**Translations.** Onkelos keeps the feminine singular and "her master" (תְּהֵי לְרִבּוֹנָהָא); since Aramaic allows either agreement, the choice is meaningful. Pseudo-Jonathan does the same and adds אַמְתָא, the rabbinic identification (Sefaria's English has "his master"; the Aramaic says "her"). Rasag's plural (יכונו) is required by Arabic word order and proves nothing; he keeps "her master" and renders the opening as "his master marries him to a woman."

## Structure
- **v. 4 is the negative image of v. 3b.**

  | v. 3b | v. 4 |
  |---|---|
  | אִם בַּעַל אִשָּׁה הוּא | אִם אֲדֹנָיו יִתֶּן־לוֹ אִשָּׁה |
  | אִשְׁתּוֹ, *his* wife | הָאִשָּׁה, *the* woman |
  | וְיָצְאָה, she goes out | תִּהְיֶה לַאדֹנֶיהָ, she stays |
  | עִמּוֹ, with him | וְהוּא… בְגַפּוֹ, he alone |

- **Frame.** v. 3 opens בְּגַפּוֹ יָבֹא בְּגַפּוֹ יֵצֵא; v. 4 closes וְהוּא יֵצֵא בְגַפּוֹ (Rashbam: כולל ואח"כ מפרש). Abarbanel assigns the two occurrences to two women, the Israelite wife and the Canaanite one.
- **Place in the paragraph.** The third אִם-sub-case of the main case in v. 2.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**"A woman" is a Canaanite bondwoman.** MdRY Nezikin 2:3, from the verse's end: "the woman and her children shall be her master's" cannot be said of a free Israelite woman.
- **Ramban against Rashi's version.** Rashi argues that a Hebrew maidservant would go free after six years or at puberty. Ramban: ואין זה מכוון כהוגן. A father-sold maidservant cannot be handed by her master to another man, and Rashi's six-year term for a self-sold woman rests on his minority view of the self-seller. Behind it: Ramban holds a woman may sell herself; Rashi and Rambam (Avadim 1:2) hold she may not.
- **Hoffmann's argument** is the strongest: the children are assigned to the master with no release date, and are of no use to him for years, so they must be his permanently, which only fits a Canaanite bondwoman's children. He adds that the Torah presupposes the foreign slave as the ordinary case (Gen 17:12, 23; Ex 12:44; Lev 22:11).
- *Assessment.* Very likely. The wording supports it: the ruling withholds "his wife" and "his children" (Words). Whether the union is a marriage the wording leaves open.

**Permission, not obligation.** MdRY Nezikin 2:1 (R. Yishmael, from v. 3a); R. Akiva (2:2) reads the clause as restricting which bondwoman. See [21:3](03_verse.md).

**MdRShbY 21:4.**
- יתן לו: כופה אותו ויבא עליה, even a kohen, and the children are slaves; only if he already has a wife and children.
- One woman, not two; not one woman for two slaves, though both are permitted with Canaanite slaves.
- האשה וילדיה: any children she bears, by him or another, are slaves.
- תהיה לאדוניה: she leaves him without a *get*, and the master may sell her and the children against the slave's wishes.

**No *get*.** MdRY Nezikin 2:6. Hoffmann: the relationship dissolves at manumission, and the master may give her to another Hebrew slave.

**The child follows the mother.** MdRY Nezikin 2:5 (R. Yishmael) argues from the bondwoman to any woman with whom betrothal does not take effect, that is, a gentile woman. *Assessment:* the verse's grammar supports the base case (Grammar and Syntax); the extension is argued, not read.

**R. Nathan** (MdRY Nezikin 2:4): from וְיָלְדָה־לוֹ, the master's own children by his bondwoman are also slaves. Malbim's reconstruction resolves a real difficulty. Kiddushin 68b says וְיָלְדוּ לוֹ applies only where betrothal takes effect, yet here וְיָלְדָה לוֹ is said of a bondwoman. For R. Nathan "his master gives him a woman" means permission to take one, even another's (cf. Temurah 30a), which is why the verse says "*her* master"; the case is a slave who owns the bondwoman himself, so the children are "to him" by ownership. *Assessment:* the switch to "her master" permits this and does not require it (Words); the clause וְיָלְדָה־לוֹ is needed for the case and is not superfluous.

### Talmud
**The child of a gentile woman follows the mother: three routes.**
- **Yevamot 23a** (R. Shimon, who expounds the reason of a verse): not from the bondwoman, but from כִּי־יָסִיר אֶת־בִּנְךָ מֵאַחֲרַי (Deut 7:4).
- **Kiddushin 68b** (the Rabbis): from כִּי־תִהְיֶיןָ לְאִישׁ… וְיָלְדוּ־לוֹ (Deut 21:15), where betrothal takes effect; this covers bondwoman and gentile together and leaves Ex 21:4 for R. Yose HaGlili's case of one who frees his bondwoman but not her child.
- **R. Yishmael** needs Ex 21:4, because on his view a *shifchah charufah* is a Canaanite bondwoman betrothed to a Hebrew slave (Kerisot 11a), so betrothal partly takes effect with her and Deut 21:15 cannot serve.

Each route belongs to a coherent position on neighbouring questions; they are not competing proof texts.

**Consequences of הָאִשָּׁה וִילָדֶיהָ** (collected by Torah Temimah): a brother from a bondwoman does not exempt from levirate marriage (Yevamot 22a); relations with a sister from a bondwoman are exempt (23a); a slave's cohabitation disqualifies from *terumah* but not as offspring (70a); and R. Tarfon, Kiddushin 69a: *mamzerim* can be purified. A *mamzer* marries a bondwoman; the child is a slave; freed, he is a free man. Torah Temimah reads this as permitted from the outset.

**Kiddushin 20a; Y. Kiddushin 1:2.** The bondwoman only for a slave with wife and children (R. Eliezer b. Ya'akov). In the Yerushalmi R. Abba bar Mamai raises the case of a kohen, is answered, and changes his mind.

**Does the Hebrew slave work at night?** Sifrei Devarim 123:1: a hired man works by day, this one day and night. MdRY: like a hired man, by day and not by night. The Yerushalmi harmonises: "night" is the master's pairing him with a Canaanite bondwoman. Torah Temimah reads Deut 15:18's מִשְׁנֶה שְׂכַר שָׂכִיר the same way.

## Halacha
- **Rambam, Avadim 3:3.** A slave sold by the court may be given a Canaanite bondwoman, by the master or the master's son after his death; he is compelled, so that she bear slaves, and she is permitted to him throughout his service. The self-seller is forbidden to her like any Israelite.
- **3:4.** Only if he has an Israelite wife and children; וְדָבָר זֶה קַבָּלָה הוּא; even a kohen.
- **3:5.** Not two bondwomen, and not one for two Hebrew slaves: שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר (שמות כא ד) "יִתֶּן לוֹ אִשָּׁה".
- **The child of a Canaanite bondwoman is a slave**: Kiddushin 68b–69a.
- **Sources**: MdRY Nezikin 2:1–6; MdRShbY 21:4; Kiddushin 20a, 68b–69a; Yevamot 23a. **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified for the Hebrew slave.
