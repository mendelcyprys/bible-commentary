# וְאִם־לִבְנ֖וֹ יִֽיעָדֶ֑נָּה כְּמִשְׁפַּ֥ט הַבָּנ֖וֹת יַעֲשֶׂה־לָּֽהּ׃

## Text
### Masoretic Notes
- **Accents.** The atnach on יִֽיעָדֶ֑נָּה divides case from rule, as in vv. 7 and 8.

### Textual Variants

## Language
Counts are from the ETCBC BHSA database unless marked otherwise.

### Words
**לִבְנוֹ, before the verb.**
- **After אִם this order is common.** Of 154 conditional clauses in Torah law (Ex 20–23; Leviticus; Numbers; Deut 12–26) opening with (ו)אִם and a finite verb: verb first 37; another constituent first 57 (objects 15, adjuncts 13, complements 12, subjects 11, time 4, place 2); negation first 27; an infinitive absolute or similar first 33. In Ex 21–22 the verb comes first after אִם only 4 times (21:8, 19; 22:2, 6).
- **vv. 8–9 front four phrases in a row**: אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ יְעָדָהּ; לְעַם נָכְרִי לֹא־יִמְשֹׁל; וְאִם־לִבְנוֹ יִיעָדֶנָּה; כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת יַעֲשֶׂה.

**יִיעָדֶנָּה.** Qal יעד (5 occurrences) assigns something the agent already controls to a time, place or purpose: מִן־הַמּוֹעֵד אֲשֶׁר יְעָדוֹ (2 Sam 20:5); שָׁם יְעָדָהּ (Jer 47:7); Mic 6:9. BDB: "assign or designate as concubine Ex 21:8, 9." Ramban and Tur HaArokh: לשון זמון. A father who gets a wife for his son from outside *takes* one: וְלָקַחְתָּ אִשָּׁה לִבְנִי לְיִצְחָק (Gen 24:4); וַיִּקַּח יְהוּדָה אִשָּׁה לְעֵר (Gen 38:6); וּקְחוּ לִבְנֵיכֶם נָשִׁים (Jer 29:6); the bride's father *gives* her: תְּנָה־אֶת־בִּתְּךָ לִבְנִי לְאִשָּׁה (2 Kgs 14:9). Ex 21:9 has neither verb: the girl is already in the house, and the master assigns her.

**Who designates.** v. 8 אֲדֹנֶיהָ אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ יְעָדָהּ and v. 9 לִבְנוֹ יִיעָדֶנָּה have the same verb and agent; the son appears only in the ל-phrase. Rashi glosses the subject as הָאָדוֹן; Mizrachi and Gur Aryeh prove it from v. 11.

**בְּנוֹ.** בֵּן can mean grandson: לָבָן בֶּן־נָחוֹר (Gen 29:5; Nahor's grandson, cf. 24:15, 29); לְבָנַי וְלִבְנֹתָי (Gen 31:28); יֵהוּא בֶן־נִמְשִׁי (2 Kgs 9:20; cf. 9:2). It carries no age.

**כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת.** "After the manner of daughters."
- **כְּמִשְׁפַּט with a class noun** means "after the manner of": כְּמִשְׁפַּט צִדֹנִים (Judg 18:7); כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַגּוֹיִם (2 Kgs 17:33). מִשְׁפַּט with a class noun is that class's due or custom: מִשְׁפַּט הַכֹּהֲנִים (Deut 18:3; 1 Sam 2:13). BDB: "custom, manner."
- **הַבָּנוֹת** with the article occurs 4 times (Gen 31:43; Ex 21:9; Jer 16:3; Song 2:2). Without a suffix בָּנוֹת can mean girls or young women (Gen 30:13; Song 6:9). A man's own daughters take the suffix: Laban must say הַבָּנוֹת בְּנֹתַי (Gen 31:43). "As he treats his own daughters" is therefore not the wording.
- **The comparison applies a known standard to a new case**: אוֹ־בֵן יִגָּח אוֹ־בַת יִגָּח כַּמִּשְׁפָּט הַזֶּה יֵעָשֶׂה לּוֹ (Ex 21:31, same code, verb and preposition); כְּמֹהַר הַבְּתוּלֹת (Ex 22:16, a customary amount never stated); כְּמִשְׁפָּטָם (Num 29). The phrase names a standard, not its content.

### Grammar and Syntax
**Clauses** (BHSA):
1. [Conj וְ] [Conj אִם] [Cmpl לִבְנוֹ] [PreO יִיעָדֶנָּה]
2. [Adju כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת] [Pred יַעֲשֶׂה] [Cmpl לָּהּ]

**Fronting does not make "his son" exclusive.** Malbim (*Ayelet HaShachar* §§91, 111, 113–114): Hebrew normally puts the verb first (§111); in a contrast resting on the nouns the noun is fronted (§113), but only when the clauses share a verb or a noun (§114); vv. 8–9 differ in both, so the fronting of לִבְנוֹ is deliberate and exact (§91): "his son, and not his brother." The argument fails on four counts:
1. After אִם a fronted constituent is common (Words), and vv. 8–9 front four in a row.
2. §114 is contradicted by fronted contrasts where noun and verb both differ, on this very subject: בִּתְּךָ לֹא־תִתֵּן לִבְנוֹ וּבִתּוֹ לֹא־תִקַּח לִבְנֶךָ (Deut 7:3); בְּנֹתֵיכֶם תִּתְּנוּ־לָנוּ וְאֶת־בְּנֹתֵינוּ תִּקְחוּ לָכֶם (Gen 34:9; also 34:21); Lev 25:44, 46; Ex 18:22.
3. On the qere, v. 8's לוֹ יְעָדָהּ and v. 9's לִבְנוֹ יִיעָדֶנָּה share the verb and differ in the ל-phrase, which by §113 is an ordinary contrast: *for himself… for his son*.
4. The halacha reads fronted nouns as examples: וְאִם־שֵׁן עַבְדּוֹ (Ex 21:27; every permanent loss of an extremity, MdRY Nezikin 9:12; Kiddushin 24a); אִם־בַּמַּחְתֶּרֶת (Ex 22:1; roof, yard and enclosure too, Sanhedrin 72b); וְאִם־בִּכְלִי נְחֹשֶׁת בֻּשָּׁלָה (Lev 6:21; אין לי אלא כלי נחושת; מנין לרבות כל שאר מתכות?, Sifra, Tzav 7:2), the verse Malbim uses to illustrate §§113–114; בַּשָּׂדֶה (Deut 22:25, 27; Sifrei Devarim 243:3).

§91 concerns instruments (בעפר, בתער, במרצע), not datives.

**Who "deals with her."** No new subject is named before יַעֲשֶׂה. The nearest agent is the master; the son is only a ל-phrase. An unmarked switch is possible, so the grammar leans to the master without deciding. The same question arises at v. 8 and v. 10.

**Translations.** Onkelos: וְאִם לִבְרֵהּ יְקַיְמִנַהּ כְּהִלְכַת בְּנַת יִשְׂרָאֵל יַעְבֵּד לַהּ; Pseudo-Jonathan: לְצֵיד בְּרֵיהּ זַמִּין יָתָהּ (the "appoint" sense of Ramban's לשון זמון), כְּהִלְכַת בְּנַת יִשְרָאֵל. Rasag: ואן זוג̇הא לאבנה, "if he marries her to his son," פכסירה אלבנאת. No translation says "his daughters," and none marks the subject of יַעֲשֶׂה.

### Septuagint
Ἐὰν δὲ τῷ υἱῷ καθομολογήσηται αὐτὴν, κατὰ τὸ δικαίωμα τῶν θυγατέρων ποιήσει αὐτῇ.

*Brenton (1851); Rahlfs (1935) has the same wording.*

- **καθομολογήσηται**: "pledges," the verb of v. 8.
- **κατὰ τὸ δικαίωμα τῶν θυγατέρων**: "according to the right of daughters." מִשְׁפָּט as a right she can claim.

### Vulgate
Sin autem filio suo desponderit eam, juxta morem filiarum faciet illi.

*Clementine Vulgate.*

- **desponderit**: "betroths."
- **juxta morem filiarum**: "after the custom of daughters." מִשְׁפָּט as custom, which leaves room for customary provision such as Ramban's *mohar*.

## Structure
- **The second sub-case** (וְאִם) of vv. 7–11, parallel to v. 8: designated for himself / for his son.
- **The status words move her from אָמָה back to בַּת.** v. 7 sells בִּתּוֹ לְאָמָה; v. 9 requires that she be treated כְּמִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת. Bekhor Shor: לא ינהג בה מנהג שפחות; Shadal: ולא תהיה קלה ובזויה בעיניו כשפחה.

## Rabbinic Interpretation
### Midrash
**His son, not his brother.** MdRY Nezikin 3:19; MdRShbY; Tractate Avadim 1:7; Sifrei Devarim 118:6. The *a fortiori* (a brother enters for levirate marriage) is blocked: the son stands in his father's place for the ancestral field, the brother does not; תִּלְמֹד לוֹמַר "וְאִם לִבְנוֹ יִיעָדֶנָּה", לִבְנוֹ יְיַעֵד וְלֹא לְאָחִיו. *Assessment:* a legal reading of what the verse names, not of its word order; nothing in the verse extends designation beyond the son.

**Only in the father's lifetime.** MdRShbY: בחיי אביו מיעדה ואין מיעדה לאחר מיתת אביו. *Assessment:* follows from the master's being the designator.

**Permission, not command.** MdRShbY: יכול גזירה? ת"ל ואם, אינו אלא רשות. *Assessment:* right, from context; R. Yishmael's rule כל "אם" ו"אם" שבתורה רשות has three exceptions (MdRY Bachodesh 11; Kaspa 1).

**No fresh betrothal.** Gur Aryeh; Siftei Chakhamim; Mizrachi; Or HaChaim; Rabbeinu Bahya: the verse says יִיעָדֶנָּה, not יִקָּחֶנָּה or יְקַדְּשֶׁנָּה. *Assessment:* supported in the plain sense (Words). That the purchase money serves as betrothal money is a further legal step, disputed by R. Yose b. R. Yehudah (Kiddushin 19b).

**"The rule for daughters."**
- **R. Yoshiyah and R. Yonathan** (MdRY Nezikin 3:20–21). R. Yoshiyah: the phrase comes to teach about her and ends up taught (בא ללמד ונמצא למד): the daughters' rule is learned from hers. The Mekhilta flags the reversal: וְכִי מַה לָּמַדְנוּ עַל מִשְׁפַּט הַבָּנוֹת? R. Yonathan: it teaches about the maidservant. Malbim explains the dispute by the referent of v. 10 and his rule (*Torah Or*, Tzav §85) that where a matter is explicit, Scripture does not compare it to something else: for R. Yoshiyah v. 10 is about the maidservant, so the comparison must teach outward; for R. Yonathan v. 10 is about a free wife, so the comparison supplies the maidservant's rights. *Assessment:* R. Yonathan's direction is the grammar of a כְּ-comparison (Words).
- **MdRShbY generalises both ways**: מה אמה עבריה היעודה אין מונעין ממנה שאר כסות ועונה — כך כל הבנות; and as a daughter betrothed by her father is liable to death for adultery and leaves only by a *get*, so is the designated maidservant.
- **Content.** She'er, kesut and onah (the Mekhilta; Rashi; Or HaChaim; Shadal; Hirsch; Sefer HaChinukh 46); a proper wedding (Chizkuni; the Netziv: the father pays, since the son has nothing); a ketubah (Hadar Zekenim; Meshekh Chokhmah). *Assessment:* the phrase names a standard, not its content. v. 10 is the nearest content, but opens a new sub-case with אִם, so it is not grammatically an explanation of v. 9.
- **Ramban's plain sense**: as a man treats his own daughters, giving her from his own like the bride-price of virgins, as with the severance gift; followed by Rabbeinu Bahya. Hirsch's English translation credits this reading to "Rambam." Cassuto: היא תהיה בביתו כאחת מבנות המשפחה. *Assessment:* "his own" is not in the words, but a customary payment fits a code that refers to unstated amounts (Ex 22:16).
- **Onkelos and Pseudo-Jonathan** specify "daughters of Israel," as do Ibn Ezra (בנות ישראל הבתולות שלא נמכרו), Chizkuni and Reggio; Hoffmann infers that the practice was simply known.

**Who is obligated** (subject of יַעֲשֶׂה). The son: Ramban (on the rabbinic reading), Sforno, Tur HaArokh, Reggio, Shadal, Hirsch. The master: Ramban's plain sense; Rabbeinu Bahya; the Netziv. Either: Ibn Ezra; Or HaChaim. *Assessment:* undecided; the grammar leans to the master.

### Talmud
- **Kiddushin 17b.** The Hebrew slave serves the son but not the daughter; a pierced slave and a Hebrew maidservant serve neither. (Y. Kiddushin 1:2 has the maidservant serve the daughter but not the son.)
- **Kiddushin 19a.** Reish Lakish: must "his son" be an adult, like the father (בנו דומיא דידיה)? Resolved from consent: אין יעוד אלא בגדול, אין יעוד אלא מדעת. Y. Kiddushin 1:2: R. Yochanan allows a minor or adult son, with or without his consent; Reish Lakish only an adult with consent. *Assessment:* the word carries no age.
- **Kiddushin 19b.** Was the purchase money betrothal money (see [21:8](08_verse.md), Talmud)?
- **Y. Kiddushin 1:2: not his grandson.** Samuel bar Abba asked why inheritance treats a grandson as a son and this law does not. "Rebbi Ze‘ira said, if anybody explains this matter, I shall pour him spiced wine." R. Nachum answered: in inheritance, where a brother or other relative can take a son's place, a grandson can too; here, where none can, neither can a grandson. *Assessment:* not from the word, since בֵּן can mean grandson; the answers are systemic.

## Halacha
- **Rambam, Avadim 4:7.** וְכֵיצַד מְיַעֲדָהּ לִבְנוֹ. אִם הָיָה בְּנוֹ גָּדוֹל וְנָתַן רְשׁוּת לְאָבִיו לְיַעֲדָהּ לוֹ הֲרֵי הָאָב אוֹמֵר לָהּ בִּפְנֵי שְׁנַיִם הֲרֵי אַתְּ מְקֻדֶּשֶׁת לִבְנִי. Rashi has the son say it; the Lechem Mishneh reconciles. Rambam's procedure fits the wording better: the master designates.
- **4:8.** If the master has died, his son cannot designate her, since she goes free at the master's death.
- **Sefer HaChinukh 46**: the content of "the rule for daughters" is she'er, kesut and onah.
- **Shulchan Arukh**: not codified.

## Modern Scholarship
### Source Analysis
- **Cassuto** reads the sub-case as concubinage for the son: she is to be one of the family's daughters.

### Ancient Near Eastern Context
- **Hammurabi §§155–156** open "If a man betroth a girl to his son" (King's 1910 translation). Which Akkadian term lies behind "betroth" has not been checked against Roth.
- **Nuzi.** Girls adopted "as *kallatu*" (daughter-in-law) "were generally given in marriage to a son of the adopter" (Justel, *VT* 72, 2022, 436).
